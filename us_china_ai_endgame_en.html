The US-China AI Endgame: Who Collects the Premium, Who Becomes a Commodity, Who Collects the Toll

This report's view (inference): to protect the premium of closed models and the debt chain behind it, the most direct move for the United States is to cut off China's compute, yet the premium can only be collected on a thin layer; China trades electricity for chips to catch up, and the harder the two fight, the more stable the two-pole world becomes; the final winners are the toll booths nobody can route around, power and licenses.

中文版

Conclusion

Jensen Huang's departure is collateral damage from the collision of two giants, the United States and China (inference): when giants collide, the bridge is torn down first.

To protect the premium of closed models and the hundreds of billions of dollars of debt behind it, the most direct move for the United States is to cut off China's compute so that open source falls a step behind. But artificial intelligence (AI) has a threshold: for most work a cheap model is enough, so the premium can only be collected on a thin layer.

Over the next three to five years China trades electricity for chips and catches up by quantity; both sides have nuclear weapons, and neither can crush the other. The harder the two fight, the further they leave everyone else behind; the path of least resistance ends in a G2 (group of two, meaning a two-pole world). Europe is flattened first. AI makes ideas no longer scarce, which discounts America's institutional edge of "there is always a challenger"; competition returns to compute, that is, chips times electricity.

Further out, world-changing breakthroughs will be released by incumbents at their own pace; such a breakthrough is also the only variable that could break the two poles. Governments can only repay debt by holding rates down and letting inflation run. The final winners are positions that get paid no matter how the rules change: toll booths, power, licenses.

The above is inference and estimate. This is foresight: it describes how the logic unfolds in the future, so the absence of present evidence is normal; milestones and counter-evidence are listed at the end.

Grades: Confirmed (company or official filing, source attached), Reported (media or institutional report), Inference (derived by logic), Estimate. Amounts are in US dollars.

Part 1: The business

Layer 1: Intelligence has a threshold (inference)

  • For the great majority of tasks an "undergraduate-level" model is enough; once a cheap model clears the bar, what remains is a price war.
  • Only a very small set of civilization-level problems (frontier science, defense, top-end engineering and finance) need the strongest model; the premium exists only in this thin layer.
  • Heavy individual users are few; vendors do not publish the number of subscribers at 100 dollars a month and above, at most one to two million worldwide (estimate).
  • Weak point: the "good enough" line may move up as tasks get harder.
  • What this layer means: valuing these companies as if all usage paid a premium rests on a premise that does not hold.

Layer 2: Chinese open source pushes prices down (reported + inference)

  • According to enterprise spending data relayed by one source (unconfirmed), for the same workload: Claude about 4,811 dollars, ChatGPT about 3,357, China's DeepSeek about 1,071, Kimi about 948.
  • On 07-30 OpenAI cut the price of its cheapest tier by 80% (reported [S6]); its chief executive officer (CEO) said he was willing to price the flagship at a quarter of the rival's, not implemented (reported [S7]).
  • Anthropic's second-quarter operating profit was 974 million, but it said full-year profitability is not assured (reported [S5]).
  • Motive (inference): turning models into a cheap good dismantles the moat of the American model companies.
  • Weak point: the price data comes from a single relay.
  • What this layer means: the commercial bulk of the model business is turning into a commodity.

Layer 3: Cut off supply, protect the premium, protect the debt chain (mostly inference)

  • A closed model can charge a high price only if it is clearly smarter; open source catches up through compute and distillation (training a small model on a strong model's outputs). Cutting compute is the most direct move. According to media relays (unconfirmed), OpenAI and Anthropic have lobbied to restrict open-weight models.
  • The debt chain: Nvidia holds about 99 billion of customer equity, has about 65 billion of cloud service commitments, and guarantees SB Energy up to 105 billion (confirmed [S3]; its link to OpenAI was not checked against the original). Anthropic listed on 11-19, raising 81.3 billion at an offer valuation of about 1.5 trillion (reported [S4]); its compute commitments total about 895.4 billion (relayed from the prospectus, original not checked), paid over many years, with the fixed part peaking at about 47.8 billion a year against second-quarter annualized revenue of about 46.4 billion (relayed). The banks, private credit funds and cloud providers that build the data centers ultimately depend on the model companies to pay (inference).
  • Cutting supply protects only the thin-layer premium; it does not close the gap between commitments and revenue. The solution this report proposes (inference, no precedent): government contracts serve as endorsement -> valuation rises -> raise money in the stock market or by issuing debt, the stock market preferred -> use the money raised to pay the compute commitments. Government contracts are not large (US Department of Defense prototype contracts are capped at 200 million per lab, reported [S15]); they are endorsement, not payment; the ones who pay in the end are the buyers in the stock market.
  • The arithmetic (estimate): at a 1.5 trillion valuation, raising 30 billion a year dilutes about 2%, and the 47.8 billion peak year about 3.2%; at half the valuation it is 4% to 6.4%. The 81.3 billion raised at listing covers about 1.7 peak years.
  • Three interests point at the same action (inference): China hawks want the cutoff, model companies want the premium, creditors want someone to pay. Background: Nvidia's latest guidance assumes no data-center compute revenue from China (confirmed [S1]).
  • The crux (inference): Jensen Huang stood exactly in the middle -- born in Taiwan, running an American company, selling chips to both sides, and promoting open source (per public reports). When giants coexist, the one in the middle is the bridge and earns the most; when giants collide, the bridge is torn down first. This needs nobody to plan it; it only needs both countries to stop needing the bridge.
  • The departure itself: on 11-12 the company announced that Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO and director, effective immediately, with no reason given; chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim CEO (confirmed [S2]). The direction of the forces behind it is the China hawks and the security establishment (inference; a direction, not an identification). Model companies are beneficiaries after the fact; there is no evidence that any company leaked material or planned anything, and benefiting is not participating.
  • It follows (inference): before the two poles take shape, other people and companies that stand in the middle and earn from both sides will face the same pressure; the mark is deep dependence on China combined with being a key link for the United States.
  • Weak points: the cutoff has gaps (Huawei Ascend chips, smuggling, renting compute overseas), and the lag is probably a few months to a year (estimate); open source is not only Chinese. Financing works both ways: when the share price falls, the ability to pay falls with it; the vital point is the model companies' share price.
  • What this layer means: protecting the premium is protecting the share price; someone pays only while the share price holds.

Part 2: Power

Layer 4: Breakthroughs will be managed (inference + history)

  • Even if the strongest model produced nearly unlimited energy, the distribution rules are set by the winners, and anything that erases the winners' advantage will not be released freely.
  • History: the civilian signal of the Global Positioning System (GPS) was deliberately degraded until 2000; strong encryption was moved off the munitions list only at the end of 1996 (confirmed [S8]).
  • The pattern: first arrange the distribution rules, then release it in a form that can be charged for; the strongest AI goes to the "general staff" first. The only accelerator is US-China competition: the pace is the faster of what one can control and what the rival forces.
  • Weak point: the precedents are state-led technologies; the AI frontier is in companies' hands and is harder to manage.
  • What this layer means: the breakthroughs the outside world sees come later than they actually happen.

Layer 7: The contest is the whole machine, not a single chip (inference)

  • Compute = chips x electricity x interconnect x memory x software; the ceiling is set by the shortest board. The two sides' short boards are exactly opposite.
  • America is strong in chips and short of power: new generation projects took a median of 61 months from interconnection request to operation in 2025 (reported [S13]).
  • China is weak in chips, with plenty of power and fast additions: its 2024 electricity generation was about 2.2 to 2.3 times America's (official statistics [S13], originals not checked). The play is electricity for chips: Huawei's CloudMatrix 384 against Nvidia's flagship rack delivers about 1.67 times the compute at about 3.86 times the power, roughly 40% of the compute per kilowatt (vendor figures, reported [S11]).
  • Who fixes its short board first: America needs years plus political bargaining to add power; China's extreme-ultraviolet lithography prototype cannot yet make usable chips (reported [S12]); under pressure it may go faster (inference).
  • Both sides have nuclear weapons; neither can crush the other.
  • Conclusion: America cannot kill China, and China cannot catch America for now; once China reaches "slightly worse but able to hold a draw", the path of least resistance is two poles, each with its sphere, with China relatively closing in over the long run.
  • Weak points: average industrial power prices in the two countries are similar (reported [S13]), so this path relies on surplus electricity, not cheap electricity; China is also weak in memory and interconnect, and losses grow with cluster size; bargaining between the two poles may involve Taiwan, which is the largest risk to "TSMC neutrality".
  • What this layer means: the contest is who fixes its short board first.

Layer 8: The harder the fight, the more stable the G2 (inference)

  • What most people miss: the harder the United States and China fight, the more stable the two poles. The contest is inner strength (compute, power, capital, scale of engineers); the harder they push, the further Europe and Japan are left behind, forming an uncrossable gap in 3 to 5 years (estimate). Other countries cannot become a third pole and can only attach to one side.
  • Europe is flattened first: its moat is decades of craft in precision manufacturing, chemicals, autos and pharmaceuticals, and China's scale of engineers plus AI can compress the learning curve. It is already happening in autos; ASML's lithography machines may be the last fortress.
  • America's greatest advantage is institutional: there is always a challenger to "slay the god". AI discounts it: when ideas can be produced in bulk by AI, the bottleneck moves from whose people are more creative to who has more compute, back to chips times electricity, where the two sides are more symmetric.
  • Weak points: the two meanings of G2 must be kept apart -- this layer means a two-pole world, which takes shape the harder they fight; it does not mean the two will reach a deal. The thin frontier layer still needs top people to set direction, so the challenger culture still matters; Europe's lithography and precision optics may be harder to catch; 3 to 5 years may be longer.
  • What this layer means: confrontation itself creates the two poles; no handshake is needed.

Part 3: Money

Layer 5: The Federal Reserve is passive (inference + reported data)

  • Long-end rates are set by the large holders of long bonds: foreign holders are retreating (the yen touched 160 intraday on 11-12); AI borrowing, new share issues and the fiscal deficit are all competing for money (inference).
  • Data (reported [S10]): the 10-year Treasury yields about 5.1%; the 30-year auction on 11-12 cleared at 5.441% with soft demand; the dollar index is near a 19-month high.
  • The large holders' script (inference): sell stocks at the year-end high -> buy long bonds at the top in yields from January to March -> once they hold enough, they want rates down -> talk of recession and rate cuts appears -> the Federal Reserve follows.
  • The path of least resistance for a government repaying debt is to hold rates down and let inflation run. Precedent: from 1942 to 1951 long-term rates were pegged below 2.5%; federal debt fell from 106% of the economy in 1946 to 22% in 1974 (confirmed [S9]).
  • Four worlds (estimate): print money to repay debt 45% to 50%, a normal cycle 25%, AI deflationary slump 15% to 20%, dollar credit crisis 10%. The most likely is the first overlapping the third: asset inflation with wage deflation.
  • Weak point: the probabilities are estimates; if Treasuries and the dollar fall together, that is the credit-crisis path.
  • What this layer means: when rates turn depends on when the large holders have bought enough.

Part 4: The outcome

Layer 6: Winners and losers (inference)

  • Winners: neutral toll booths -- at the network layer, for example Cloudflare (charges by usage, takes no cut of transaction value, bets on no particular model), at the hardware layer, for example TSMC (whoever wins places orders with it); power and the grid; licensed businesses.
  • Losers: software priced per seat; payment intermediaries that take a cut of transaction value (agents, meaning AI programs that carry out tasks on their own, will route around them automatically); chip makers that lose the China market; the commercial bulk of the model companies turns into a commodity, and their most valuable capability becomes state contracts.
  • The test: the fee is far below the value of the service and far below the cost of switching channels -> it survives; otherwise it will sooner or later be routed around.
  • Weak point: TSMC's neutrality is constrained by the Taiwan question.
  • What this layer means: the winners are the positions nobody can route around.

Layer 9: The part that cannot be computed -- emergence (inference, cannot be quantified)

  • The US-China race will pour massive compute and agents into basic science, and may throw up breakthroughs nobody expected, in materials, energy, biology and fundamental physics. It is like buying lottery tickets: the more you buy, the higher the chance of a win. It cannot be quantified, and what exactly it will be cannot be predicted.
  • It is the only thing that could break the two poles; but by layer 4, the side that gets it first will put it into the "general staff" first, so the outside world sees it late.
  • Timing (estimate): it has to wait until agents can do research independently; a dense run of results is possible only after 2028. Signal: a rising share of research results proposed by AI and adopted.
  • Weak point: it may not come for a long time.
  • What this layer means: the only unknown; all one can do is watch the signal.

The opposing views

  1. America keeps a decisive lead. Frontier models, allied compute and capital markets mean China cannot reach the frontier with quantity and electricity. This report's reply: the frontier is only a thin layer (layer 1); most usage is decided by "good enough" and cost.
  2. The AI bubble bursts as a whole and both sides lose. Revenue cannot support the investment, the debt chain breaks, investment stops. This report's reply: the system will step in to protect the debt chain (layers 3 and 5); but this is not ruled out, it is the deflationary slump and the credit crisis among the four worlds, together 25% to 30% (estimate).
  3. Open source wins outright and nobody collects a premium. Models become a public good like electricity. This report's reply: that is actually best for the toll booths (layer 6); the cutoff can slow open source, but not for long.

Closing

When giants coexist, the bridge earns the most; when they collide, the bridge is torn down first (inference). The premium stays in the thin layer, the bulk goes to commodity prices, and the toll goes to the positions nobody can route around.
