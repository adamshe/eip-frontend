1. Conclusion

This report's view: sorted by economic position, the friends of Jensen Huang (the former chief executive below) are the Taiwanese foundry and Korean memory makers that collect rent alongside Nvidia, plus a few public voices and the employees who back him; his enemies are the cloud giants who pay the rent, the US hardware rivals who compete for it, and the national-interest side. The friends are very weak and the enemies very strong (inferred).

The rent is Nvidia's pricing power. Second-quarter gross margin was 75.0% (reported): of every USD 100 a customer pays, Nvidia keeps USD 75 of gross profit (calculated). The money that pays the rent is concentrated: three direct customers together made up 54% of first-quarter revenue (calculated). The suppliers' orders and prices rest on that pricing power: Nvidia is about 22% of TSMC's 2026 revenue (reported, analyst estimate), and Nvidia is expected to give about two thirds of its next-generation HBM (high-bandwidth memory) demand to SK hynix (reported).

Today the balance of forces is one-sided. The friends' support stops at words: public posts and paraphrased remarks, with no vote on the Nvidia board, no capital committed and no procedural power (inferred). The enemies hold money, orders and procedure: the rent payers keep paying while pushing their own chips at TSMC (reported), and three House committees have sent two rounds of letters and asked for a closed-door briefing on 2026-11-18 (confirmed).

For a comeback, a return to Nvidia gets harder. The other report published on 2026-11-14 estimates a return to Nvidia at about 4% by the end of 2027 and about 7% by the end of 2029 (inferred); the rent-chain view supports that direction. For founding or leading a company valued at USD 10 billion or more, the rent-sharing suppliers and capital providers are ready-made friends, yet this report judges the path very unlikely, below that report's estimate; the most likely outcome is retirement or remaining a shareholder only (inferred).

2. Method: the test is who collects the rent and who pays it

This report sorts enemies and friends by a single test: economic position, that is, whether a party collects or pays rent on Nvidia's pricing power (set by this report).

Rent = pricing power, measured by gross margin. For the second quarter of fiscal 2027 (ended 2026-07-26), gross margin was 75.0% on both the GAAP (US generally accepted accounting principles) and non-GAAP basis, with third-quarter guidance of 74.0% plus or minus 0.5 percentage points (reported, distributed copy of the company release).

Three kinds of parties are sorted three ways. Parties on the rent chain (payers, competitors for the rent, rent-sharing suppliers) are sorted by economic position; individuals off the chain (public supporters, employees) by their public stance; the national-interest side by how its interest relates to the pricing power (set by this report).

The difference from the other report published the same day is the test. That report sorted parties by their interest in his return or in a new venture, put the cloud firms on both sides (dependent on Nvidia's products while building substitutes) and put the supply chain in the middle; this report sorts by who collects and who pays, so the cloud firms are payers and enemies, and the foundry and memory makers are rent-sharing suppliers and friends (set by this report).

The company's announcement gave no reason for the departure, and this report does not discuss reasons carried in media reports.

Suppliers are paid out of Nvidia's cost of revenue, not out of the gross profit. Of every USD 100 a customer pays, about USD 25 outside the gross profit is cost of revenue (calculated); the suppliers' tie to the pricing power runs through order volume and price: a high gross margin is what lets Nvidia pay the prices of leading-edge wafers, advanced packaging and HBM and lock in capacity ahead of time (inferred).

TSMC also makes the payers' own chips. Google TPU Ironwood, Amazon Trainium 3 and Microsoft Maia 200 are all reported on TSMC's 3-nanometer process, and several generations of Meta MTIA are in progress (reported, single aggregator source).

In the supply chain the principal contradiction is the shared pricing power and the secondary one is buying and selling (inferred). Nvidia paying TSMC and the HBM makers, and the bargaining between them, is the secondary side; the higher Nvidia prices and the more it ships, the fuller the order books and the firmer the quotes at TSMC and the HBM makers, which is the principal side (inferred). The largest support is not a statement but who gets capacity first: Nvidia has booked more than half of TSMC's 2026 advanced packaging capacity (reported, estimate); on the HBM side Nvidia is expected to give about two thirds of its HBM4 demand to SK hynix (reported), while the order in which SK hynix allocates capacity was not found and priority for Nvidia is inferred. TSMC's pricing power is built around Nvidia (inferred). The relationship is not black and white: they collect from every customer, the cloud giants with their own chips included, but Nvidia comes first (inferred).

5. Balance of forces

This report's view: the friends are very weak and the enemies very strong; the friends' support is verbal, while the enemies hold money, orders and procedure (inferred).

The friends are weak in three places. First, support stops at words: the public supporters' statements are posts and paraphrases (reported), with no vote on the Nvidia board, no capital committed and no procedural power (inferred). Second, the rent-sharing suppliers are silent: no public statement on his departure since 2026-11-08 by TSMC, SK hynix, Samsung or Foxconn was found; they hold no seat on the Nvidia board and also supply the cloud giants who pay the rent, so taking a public side costs them alone (inferred); their largest support is capacity priority, and today that priority goes to Nvidia the company (inferred). Third, employees have no collective vote: their equity value sits inside the company, unvested equity keeps them in place, and no named or collective public statement was found (inferred).

The enemies are strong in three places. Money and orders: three direct customers made up 54% of first-quarter revenue and five direct customers 70% of accounts receivable on 2026-07-26 (calculated; mostly cloud giants behind them, inferred); the payers keep paying while pushing their own chips on TSMC 3-nanometer (reported). Procedure: three House committees, two rounds of letters, a requested closed-door briefing on 2026-11-18 (confirmed), and the committees hold subpoena power (inferred). Votes: the board accepted his resignation as president, chief executive and director and appointed a board chair and an interim chief executive (confirmed); he holds about 3.58% of the shares (reported, as of 2026-03-23), and the company has a single class of common stock (reported).

6. United front

This report's view: on the path back to Nvidia no front can be formed; on the path of a new company the friends are ready-made, yet this report judges that path very unlikely (inferred).

Back to Nvidia: unite the employees who back him; win over the institutional holders, whose votes decide the board, with the ten largest institutions holding 33.45% together (calculated, the other report published on 2026-11-14); no party can be isolated, since the payers hold more than half of revenue (calculated; inferred) and Congress holds procedure (confirmed) (inferred). The rent-sharing suppliers have no votes to cast on this path (inferred). The rent-chain view therefore supports the other report's direction: a return to Nvidia at about 4% by the end of 2027 and about 7% by the end of 2029 (inferred).

Founding or leading a company valued at USD 10 billion or more (inferred): unite the rent-sharing suppliers (leading-edge wafers, advanced packaging and HBM) and the capital providers (sovereign funds and venture capital); win over the cloud giants as buyers of a second source, and engineers (bound by non-solicitation terms, which were not found); isolate the congressional line by choosing a direction that does not overlap with controlled chips (inferred).

This path is very unlikely, and the reason lies in his own balance sheet (inferred). He holds about 3.58% of the shares, worth about USD 144.7 billion at the 2026-11-12 close (calculated, the other report published on 2026-11-14); a USD 10 billion company, even if he owned all of it, would be less than one tenth of that stake (calculated) and adds little; the most likely outcome is retirement or remaining a shareholder only (inferred). This is lower than the other report's estimate for this path (about 10% by 2027-12-31 and 25% by 2029-12-31, inferred); this report judges that estimate too high and gives no number of its own.

8. What this means for readers following the company

This report's view: the person has left and the rent chain keeps running; what matters is the size of the rent, not who holds the seat (inferred).

The size of the rent shows in gross margin. Where gross margin lands against the 74.0% guidance (reported) is the most direct reading of the pricing power.

The ceiling on the rent shows in the payers' substitution. The pace at which the cloud giants' own chips go into production on TSMC 3-nanometer (reported) decides how much the payers are still willing to pay.

The other side of the rent shows in the suppliers' priority. Who gets advanced packaging and HBM capacity first (reported, estimate) is the supply-chain reading of the pricing power; if that priority loosens, the change in pricing power shows up in the supply chain first (inferred).

The effect of the congressional line on the company depends on whether it moves from the person to the company (inferred). This report gives no probability of its own.
