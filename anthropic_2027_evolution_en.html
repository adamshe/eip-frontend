Anthropic 2027-2030: How It Evolves

The most likely path is about USD 245.0 billion of revenue in 2030 (inferred, probability about 55%), not the reported USD 685.0 billion; the roughly USD 451.6 billion of compute commitments that fall due before 2030 can be paid, so the risk is in valuation, not in repayment, and the first signal will be the first post-listing quarterly report in early 2027.

Conclusion

The most likely path is revenue of about USD 110 billion in 2027 and about USD 245.0 billion in 2030 (inferred, probability about 55%), not the USD 685.0 billion that media attribute to Goldman Sachs. The roughly USD 451.6 billion of compute commitments that fall due before 2030 can be paid (USD 900.9 billion is the total commitment; the rest falls due after 2030). The real risk is not repayment but valuation.

First, the present. Revenue for the first nine months was USD 33.2 billion and USD 17.0 billion in the third quarter alone, but quarter-on-quarter growth has already slowed from 144% in the second quarter to 47%. In the first half of this year, every USD 1 spent on compute (training plus inference) brought back about USD 1.8 of revenue. The 85% gross margin looks good, but it counts only the small inference slice; training compute is booked in research and development. Next, 2027. The listing and the concurrent private placement bring in about USD 82.0-88.0 billion of new cash (inferred); added to the existing USD 131.9 billion, year-end cash is about USD 215.0 billion. Compute commitments due by the end of 2030 total USD 451.6 billion, spread at about USD 80.0, 105, 120 and 130 billion per year (inferred). Contracted compute grows faster than base-line revenue, so in 2027 the revenue earned per USD 1 of compute falls from 1.8 to about 1.4, and margins are squeezed first. As long as that ratio does not fall below about 1.33, no outside funding is needed. Even on the worse path where revenue stalls at USD 90-120 billion, the four-year cumulative gap is about USD 120 billion, the largest single year is 2030 at about USD 40 billion, cash can cover it, and USD 186.2 billion of contracts can be terminated or are conditional and can be cut.

Further out. For the Goldman line to hold, three things must happen together: single-year compute in 2030 of about USD 370-480 billion (about 3 times the contracts already signed, which suppliers would have to finance); enterprises moving payroll budgets to models at scale, turning white-collar employment negative; and open-source price pressure not eroding unit prices. Today's data supports none of the three: US professional and business services employment is still up 269,000 year on year; the open-source share of enterprise token usage (a token is the unit by which a model measures text) rose from 23% to 34% within a year; and the lenders financing AI compute are already demanding a high price, with Oracle's credit default swap (default insurance) price at an 18-year high and CoreWeave's 2032 bond yielding 14.5%.

On rates, naughty boss's hypothesis (hike first to suppress inflation and long-end yields, then cut all the way, with the bubble kept alive by cuts) has evidence for its first half: after the September hike, the 10-year Treasury yield fell from an intraday 5.364% on October 7, closing at 4.86% on 11-06 and about 4.99% after the 11-10 Consumer Price Index (CPI) release (tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield). The second half does not hold as a general rule: the Federal Reserve's own projections show no cut through the end of 2027, and the cuts that began in 2001 and 2007 did not save bubbles that had already burst. Rate cuts keep a bubble alive only while earnings do not break.

So the probabilities of the three cases (inferred) are: Goldman line 15%, base line 55%, worse line 30%. The ceiling of the listing valuation is about USD 1.02 trillion, roughly equal to the probability-weighted value of about USD 1.1 trillion across the three cases; the upside is in the Goldman line (USD 2-3 trillion at the end of 2027), and the downside is in the worse line (USD 300-500 billion). The first data to reveal the direction is the quarter-on-quarter growth rate and compute spending in the first post-listing quarterly report in February-March 2027.

Sources for the factual numbers in this conclusion are in the sections below and the source list at the end; items marked inferred or estimate are calculated in this article.

1. Big picture: rates, inflation, Treasuries, and what each side wants

On September 16 the Federal Reserve raised rates by 25 basis points to 3.75-4.00%, a unanimous 12-0 vote and the first hike since 2023 [11]. The median of its Summary of Economic Projections (the committee members' rate forecasts published each quarter, hereafter the dot plot) is 4.1% at the end of 2026 and stays there through the end of 2027 [11]. Chair Warsh took office on May 22, 2026 [45]. His stated approach is to shrink the balance sheet so that rate cuts become "needed" rather than "wanted" [19]. The path of least resistance is to buy back inflation credibility with one or two hikes, then lower the short end through a combination of "balance-sheet reduction in exchange for cuts", while leaving long-end yields to the market.

Inflation leaves no room for cuts. October CPI rose 0.4% month on month, core 0.3% [13]; the October year-on-year figure was not published because data for October 2025 is missing due to the shutdown, so the latest visible year-on-year figure is 3.7% for September [14]. The market pricing of another hike at the December 8-9 meeting was, as of 11-01, about 72% in secondary-source data; the figure after the CPI release has not been rechecked (secondary source, only one source [18]).

The Treasury wants low funding costs. The fiscal 2026 deficit is about USD 2 trillion [20], and net interest spending is about USD 1 trillion (secondary source, unverified). The November 4 quarterly refunding kept coupon issuance at USD 125 billion (3-year USD 58 billion, 10-year USD 42 billion, 30-year USD 25 billion) [21]. The interest burden gives a political motive for cuts, but cutting with inflation at 3.7% would raise the long-end inflation premium, which is why Warsh hikes first.

Long-end yields have already come off their highs: the 10-year was an intraday 5.364% on October 7 [17], closed at 4.86% on 11-06, and was about 4.99% after the 11-10 CPI release (tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield) [16]; the 30-year was about 5.70% in October, a 24-year high. For a company valued at USD 1 trillion whose profits have not yet arrived, high rates mean that almost all the valuation comes from distant cash flows, so each step up in the discount rate compresses the multiple a step.

The timing for the underwriters and the company is not random either. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead, with a price range of USD 70-78 and an offering of 825 million shares plus 123.75 million over-allotment shares [1]. Media report pricing around November 18 and listing on the 19th [6], ahead of the December rate meeting and the year-end index rebalancing. The float is only about 6.3% of the 13.087 billion shares outstanding after the offering (self-calculated [1]); a small float combined with the Nasdaq-100 fast-entry rule for large new listings (market-cap comparison on the 7th trading day after listing, eligible after 15 trading days [44]) will create passive buying in the first month. For the company, locking in more than USD 82 billion of permanent capital while growth is still above 40% and before the hiking cycle has pressed on risk assets is the path of least resistance; the window is closing, so it issues now.

2. Industry: AI spending, open source, compute supply and demand

Demand is rising, but where the money comes from is the key. Menlo Ventures counts enterprise AI spending of USD 176.8 billion in 2026 through September, 5.4 times the USD 33 billion of all of 2025; 55% of it comes from new budgets and 22% from existing software budgets [25]. The same firm estimates Anthropic takes 55% of enterprise large-model API spending, OpenAI 31%, and Google 9% (secondary source [26]). Gartner forecasts worldwide IT spending of USD 7.35 trillion in 2027 (+15%), of which software is about USD 1.68 trillion, data centers USD 1.37 trillion (+55.5%), and IT services USD 1.63 trillion [24].

Enterprises' path of least resistance is multiple suppliers, pilot first and scale later, and handing cheap work to open source. In a KPMG survey about half of large enterprises narrowed, delayed or paused AI agent projects because costs ran ahead of value [36]; Forrester expects enterprises to push about a quarter of planned AI spending into 2027 [37]. Open-weight models account for 34% of enterprise token usage, up from 23% a year ago, and respondents expect 41% a year from now (ETR, Enterprise Technology Research, survey of 200 companies [27]). But usage is not money: on Vercel's AI gateway in June, Anthropic had only 32% of tokens but 61% of spending [28].

The motive of the compute suppliers is to lock in demand and finance their own customers. In step with this listing, Google and Nvidia each subscribed USD 10 billion, AMD USD 2.5 billion, Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft USD 1 billion each, and TSMC USD 0.5 billion, a total of USD 26 billion of Class N shares [1]; these companies are also Anthropic's compute sellers, so the money circles back into their revenue. The three clouds' third quarters: AWS revenue of USD 45.98 billion, up 39.3% year on year, with backlog (contracts signed but not yet recognized) of USD 578 billion (sourced: fierce-network.com, ciodive.com [38]); Google Cloud USD 30.7 billion (+103%, single secondary source, unconfirmed); Azure USD 33.2 billion (+48%, secondary source, absolute figure disputed, unconfirmed). Credit in the supply chain has begun to charge a price: Oracle's 5-year default insurance price is about 203 basis points, an 18-year high (August, secondary source [22]); CoreWeave's 9.625% bond due 2032 yielded 14.5% on October 13 (secondary source [23]).

3. Anthropic revenue: two independent paths and reconciliation

Known facts [1]: revenue was USD 386 million in 2024 and USD 4.585 billion in 2025; in 2026, USD 4.731 billion in the first quarter, USD 11.555 billion in the second and USD 16.956 billion in the third, USD 33.242 billion for the first nine months. Third-quarter growth was +46.7% quarter on quarter, against +144% in the second. Net revenue retention (how many times more existing customers pay this year than last year) is about 900%. No single customer exceeds 10%, and the top five are about 25%. Sales through cloud providers' marketplaces are booked as revenue on a gross basis, accounting for 47% of 2025 revenue and 44% of the first nine months of 2026. Operating cash flow for the first nine months was USD 6.569 billion; less capital expenditure of USD 0.925 billion it is USD 5.644 billion, and less prepayments for leased assets of USD 1.955 billion it is USD 3.689 billion (self-calculated). First-half cost of revenue was USD 2.4 billion, with an 85% gross margin against 12% in the same period last year [3]; first-nine-month cost of revenue was about USD 4.812 billion (secondary source [4]). Total first-half compute spending was USD 8.9 billion, of which only USD 2.4 billion went into cost of revenue and the rest was training, booked in research and development [5]. Annualized revenue: above USD 65 billion at the end of July [9], and about USD 80 billion at the end of October (third-party estimate [10]).

Full-year 2026: if the fourth quarter grows 20-25% quarter on quarter, the quarter is about USD 20.3-21.2 billion and the full year about USD 53.5-54.5 billion (inferred).

Demand side (who pays, and out of which budget). Full-year 2026 enterprise AI spending is about USD 240 billion (inferred, extrapolated from USD 176.8 billion for the first nine months), of which Anthropic's roughly USD 53.5 billion is about one fifth. By 2030, if enterprise AI spending rises to about one tenth of global IT spending, it is about USD 900 billion-1 trillion (inferred; IT spending extrapolated at about 8% a year to about USD 9.3 trillion); the model layer takes about 40% of that and Anthropic about 40% of the model layer, so the enterprise portion is about USD 150-160 billion. Adding non-enterprise parts such as personal subscriptions and developers, the total is about USD 190 to 230 billion (enterprise portion USD 150 to 160 billion, back-calculated from enterprises being about 80% of the total; estimate). This is slightly below the roughly USD 240 billion derived from "who pays", with its upper end close to it. Where the money comes from: by Menlo's proportions, about one fifth from software budgets (squeezing software companies) and more than half from new budgets; for new budgets to keep growing, wages must be replaced, which is checked in Section 9.

Supply side (how many dollars of revenue each USD 1 of compute brings). In the first half of this year the ratio was about 1.83 (self-calculated [3][5]). The prospectus states total commitments of USD 900.9 billion, USD 70.7 billion in the next 12 months, USD 451.6 billion by the end of 2030, with the rest mostly in 2031-2045, plus USD 150-175 billion of arrangements with no fixed amount [1]. Spreading the contracts across years from the two totals, the 12-month USD 70.7 billion and the USD 451.6 billion through 2030, gives about USD 15.0 billion in the fourth quarter of 2026, USD 80.0 billion in 2027, USD 105 billion in 2028, USD 120 billion in 2029 and USD 130 billion in 2030 (inferred; the prospectus gives year-by-year figures only for USD 313.8 billion of purchase commitments: USD 28.4 billion in 2027, USD 43.5 billion in 2028, USD 46.9 billion in 2029 and USD 44.2 billion in 2030 [1]). Multiplying contracted compute by today's 1.83, 2027 can support about USD 146 billion of revenue and 2030 about USD 238 billion (inferred).

Reconciliation. The two paths come close but do not coincide in 2030 (demand side USD 190-230 billion, slightly below the supply side of about USD 238 billion; the base line's USD 245 billion takes the upper end implied by contracted compute, which is on the optimistic side from the demand side) and diverge in 2027 (demand side about USD 110-125 billion after growth decay, supply side about USD 146 billion). This has two implications. First, when signing the contracts the company bet on a faster 2027 than the base line; if demand does not keep up, the surplus compute will be used to train larger models and will not sit idle, but the revenue per USD 1 of compute will fall from 1.8 to about 1.4 and margins will be squeezed. Second, the ceiling implied by existing contracts is around USD 240 billion, and Goldman's USD 685.0 billion would require signing 2-3 times more compute.

6. Open-source price pressure: how far unit prices fall and how much usage must grow

The downward force on unit prices is real. The inference price to reach the same capability level falls 9x to 900x per year, depending on the capability threshold (Epoch AI, 2025-03-12, https://epoch.ai/blog/llm-inference-price-trends) [30]. List prices of open-source and Chinese models are about 1/2 to 1/6 of Anthropic's mid-tier model: Anthropic Sonnet 5.5 costs USD 2 per million input tokens and USD 10 per million output tokens, DeepSeek v4-pro at peak pricing USD 1.32 / 3.96, and Tongyi Qianwen qwen3.7-plus USD 0.4 / 1.6 [29].

But frontier model unit prices did not follow costs down in 2026: Anthropic kept Sonnet at USD 2 / 10 and cancelled a planned increase to USD 3 / 15 [32]; xAI raised its top tier by 140% in August [31]; Anthropic's gross margin rose from 12% in the first half of last year to 85% in the first half of this year [3], which indicates that most of the cost decline stayed with the company.

Inferred: from 2026 to 2030, Anthropic's average revenue per token falls 25-40% a year (high-end usage migrating to cheaper tiers, plus open source taking the price-sensitive part), reaching 1/8 to 1/3 of today's level after four years. To reach the base line of USD 245.0 billion (about 4.6 times), token usage must grow about 15-36 times; to reach the Goldman line of USD 685.0 billion (about 13 times), about 40-100 times. If the open-source share of enterprise tokens rises from 34% to 50%, closed-source's share of total usage falls from 66% to 50%, and the required multiples are multiplied by about 1.3 again.

7. Testing the interest-rate hypothesis

Naughty boss's hypothesis has two parts: first hike to suppress inflation and the long end; then cut all the way, using cuts to lift valuation multiples and keep the bubble alive.

Supporting evidence: the September hike was unanimous [11], October CPI at 0.4% month on month is still hot [13], the probability of another hike in December was, as of 11-01, about 72% in secondary-source data, with the figure after the CPI release not rechecked (secondary source, only one source [18]), so the Fed is indeed taking the "hike first" step; after the hike the 10-year fell from an intraday high of 5.364%, closing at 4.86% on 11-06 and about 4.99% after the 11-10 CPI release (tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield) [16][17], which indicates that credibility bought back the long end; and the fiscal constraint of a roughly USD 2 trillion deficit and roughly USD 1 trillion of interest [20], together with Warsh's stated "balance-sheet reduction in exchange for cuts" approach [19], gives a motive for later cuts.

Opposing evidence: the dot plot median stays at 4.1% through the end of 2027, so the Fed itself has not drawn cuts [11]; balance-sheet reduction raises the long-end term premium, cutting the short end is not cutting the long end, and growth-stock valuations depend mainly on the long end; historically, during the hiking period from June 1999 to May 2000 the Nasdaq kept rising until March 2000, and even the 475 basis points of cuts in 2001 did not stop the decline; cuts began in September 2007 and 2008 crashed anyway (public record). The precondition for cuts to keep a bubble alive is that earnings do not break; once earnings or credit break first, cuts only slow the fall and do not sustain the bubble.

Judgment: the sequence "hike first, cut later" partly holds, with the earliest cut in the second half of 2027 (inferred, based on the dot plot and inflation); "cuts keep the bubble alive" as a general rule does not hold. The direct implication for Anthropic: the listing and the first lock-up expiry (around May 2027) both fall in a window when rates are still high, so the valuation multiple has no cut to support it and can only rest on growth.

8. If revenue follows the USD 240 billion line: who is hurt first, who benefits

The first hurt are the compute suppliers who built capacity ahead of time for the Goldman line and borrowed at high rates. Among Anthropic's contracts, Nscale is up to USD 44.6 billion, Lambda about USD 41.3 billion and SpaceXAI up to USD 120.5 billion (cancellable on 90 days' notice) [1]; if second-tier suppliers like these borrowed to build capacity for higher demand, they will face under-utilization first on the base line; CoreWeave's 14.5% bond yield [23] and Oracle's 18-year-high default insurance price [22] have already priced in part of this.

Chip makers diverge. Anthropic's commitments include Broadcom leases of USD 161.2 billion, AMD of more than USD 20 billion, Google of at least USD 121.3 billion, Amazon of at least USD 110 billion, and Microsoft of USD 47.2-49.2 billion [1]; custom chips (such as Google tensor processing units made through Broadcom) are a rising share of its compute, so on the base line Nvidia's share at this customer would be diluted, though the total keeps growing.

The cloud vendors still collect on the base line, because the contracts exist; the risk is how large a share of their backlog comes from a few model companies, and that figure is not public.

Software companies are one source of the money: about one fifth of enterprise AI spending comes from existing software budgets [25], and on the base line the money shifting from software budgets to Anthropic by 2030 is about USD 30-40 billion a year (inferred), with seat-priced software squeezed first.

In white-collar industries, "not hiring" comes before "laying off". Information sector employment is -4.2% year on year [33]; professional and business services job openings were 1.305 million in July and 1.186 million in August [34]; but employment in that sector is still up 269,000 year on year [33], and the unemployment rate for college graduates is 2.6% [33]; layoffs explicitly attributed to AI this year through October total 121,000 [35]. The base line needs new positions to be frozen, not mass layoffs.

Beneficiaries: enterprise customers who get cheap intelligence (most of the benefit of falling unit prices stays with buyers), the power and storage chains, and the three big clouds that can still collect full rent on the base line.

9. Zero-sum check

Can revenue growing 13 times and open source taking half of usage both be true? Yes, but the price is usage growing about 50 to 130 times (the 40 to 100 times of Section 6 multiplied by 1.3), happening while unit prices fall 25-40% a year; stacked together, they require enterprises' total AI budget to approach USD 1 trillion in 2030, leaving only the third question.

Can a surge in enterprise AI spending and no white-collar layoffs both be true? Today yes: USD 176.8 billion of AI spending [25] is under one tenth of the roughly USD 2 trillion wage bill of US professional and business services (estimate: 22.6 million people x about USD 90,000), a figure that compares global spending with one country's wage bill and is meaningful only as an order of magnitude, and can be borne by new budgets and profits. On the Goldman line it cannot: Anthropic alone would collect about USD 550 billion from enterprises in 2030 (inferred, with enterprises at about 80%), and adding OpenAI's own planned USD 280 billion of 2030 revenue [43] and other model companies, the total approaches half of this wage bill (this compares global revenue with one country's wage bill, the bases are not equivalent, and only the order of magnitude is meaningful), so without layoffs the money cannot be found. So the Goldman line and "white-collar employment keeps growing" are mutually exclusive after 2028.

Morningstar's two figures are also mutually exclusive: a USD 1.7 trillion valuation equal to 13 times 2027 revenue implies 2027 revenue of about USD 131 billion; yet a five-year compound growth rate of 47% starting from about USD 52 billion in 2026 gives only about USD 76 billion in 2027. They cannot both hold (the sources of both figures were not found).

12. Counter-evidence points: if this data appears, the main judgment is wrong

  1. Quarter-on-quarter revenue growth still above 25% in the fourth quarter of 2026 or the first quarter of 2027: growth is not decaying, and the probability of the Goldman line should be raised.
  2. The company signs more than USD 300 billion of new compute contracts with fixed terms in 2027: the company itself is betting on the Goldman line.
  3. Revenue per USD 1 of compute rises above 2 in each quarter of 2027 instead of falling: pricing power is stronger than assumed, and the base line is too low.
  4. US professional and business services employment turns negative year on year for three consecutive months: wage replacement has begun, and the demand side of the Goldman line has money.
  5. Open source's share of enterprise token usage falls back below 30%: price pressure is weaker than assumed.
  6. In the opposite direction: two consecutive quarters of quarter-on-quarter growth below 8%, or the company starts exercising termination rights: the worse line is on.
  7. Inflation falls below 3% and cuts begin in the first half of 2027 while earnings growth holds: valuation multiples have support, and the end-2027 valuation should move up to the top of the range.

13. Gaps with market claims

Confirmed but not discussed enough:

  • Growth is decaying quickly: quarter-on-quarter 144% to 47% [1]; the market mostly quotes annualized revenue (USD 65 billion, USD 80 billion), and the annualized basis hides the decay.
  • The 85% gross margin covers only inference: of USD 8.9 billion of compute in the first half, only USD 2.4 billion went into cost of revenue [5]; on the full basis each USD 1 of compute brings about USD 1.8 of revenue, and the contracts mean this ratio will fall in 2027.
  • 44% of revenue is booked gross through cloud marketplaces [1], which overstates it when compared with companies that book net.
  • Of the USD 900.9 billion of commitments, USD 186.2 billion carries termination rights or conditions [1]: the commitment is softer than the headline, and part of the risk is passed to suppliers.
  • Supply-chain credit is already charging a price [22][23], and the roughly USD 500 billion of new capacity financing the Goldman line needs will cost more.
  • The Fed dot plot shows no cut through the end of 2027 [11], the opposite of the "cuts soon" narrative.

Loudly claimed but empty of data:

  • Goldman's 2026-2030 revenue and USD 33.6 billion of 2030 free cash flow: neither the original nor the source of the reports was found.
  • Morningstar's 47% compound growth and 13 times: source not found, and the two numbers contradict each other (Section 9); the Morningstar page shows only the USD 1.7 trillion headline [41].
  • "USD 437 billion non-cancellable, USD 213 billion can be left unpaid": not found in the prospectus, which states USD 186.2 billion with termination rights or conditions.
  • "57% of pilot enterprises switch if they save 25%, 93% say closed-source usage is also rising": source not found.
  • Annualized revenue of USD 80 billion is a third-party estimate [10] that the company has not confirmed; OpenAI's annualized revenue of about USD 70 billion has only a single source [42].
