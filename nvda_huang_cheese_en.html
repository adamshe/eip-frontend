1. Conclusion

Measured in dollars, the answer to whose cheese (whose profits or interests his actions cut into) Nvidia moved most under Jensen Huang is the five cloud giants. Over the last four quarters the five paid Nvidia about $140.4 billion to $151.6 billion of gross profit in all (calculated, [6][8][25]). The dollar figure in the labs' row was not found, so the ranking covers only the parties that can be calculated.

Nvidia's gross margin is above benchmark and the excess is positive; this can be established (calculated). Whether it became a heavy burden on the five is graded separately on two measures. Counting all of the gross profit, it equals 20.3% to 21.9% of the five's capital spending over the same period (calculated, [24]), which lands at doubtful. Counting only the part above benchmark, it is $25.4 billion to $49.6 billion, 3.7% to 7.2% (calculated, [9][10][11][12][13]), which lands at not supported. No public record of using allocation or bundling to press buyers was found, so this is not graded. The cycle and demand outrunning supply can also explain the high gross margin; attributing the excess gross profit to him reaches doubtful at most.

That he supported open source is established: Nvidia released open-weight models. The investment pledges that can be seen went to closed-source labs, so supporting open more than closed is not supported. Whether the support made open models stronger is doubtful.

A fatal blow from Chinese open-weight models to the two labs had not happened as of today, and the reading points the other way: neither the fatal nor the severe-injury threshold was crossed, and neither lab's revenue fell. The price-pressure threshold lacks a comparable price from a year earlier; not found, not graded. This is the reading on 2026-11-19, not a verdict on the future. Whether it happens later depends on two links: whether the capability gap between open and closed models narrows to good enough for enterprises (the public index gap is now 19 points, [55] calculated); and whether enterprise money follows usage (open-source models now take 19% of enterprise usage but only 4% of enterprise spending, [66] confirmed).

The claim that the espionage allegation was only a pretext, not the decisive reason for his departure, is doubtful, and capped there. This report does not judge whether the allegation is true. The absence of an indictment does not mean an absence of evidence, and it carries almost no evidential weight for the pretext claim either, since a true allegation could equally be used as the reason. The pretext claim rests on the premise that the allegation has no public evidence, so once a public indictment appears it drops to not supported (the evidence at hand would no longer support it; it would not be falsified). The US Justice Department reportedly has a criminal investigation into the Singapore company Megaspeed ([75] reported); this investigation producing a public indictment before 2027-12-31: about 15% (inferred).

Since he left, the deviations that can be confirmed as appearing only after his departure number zero; the link that tests the whose-cheese explanation currently has an empty reading. Each of three candidates has a problem: fourth-quarter gross margin guidance fell to 71.0% to 72.0% ([4] confirmed), but the reasons the company gave predate his departure ([5] reported); shipments to China have stopped ([86] reported), but when they stopped was not found and may predate his departure; the board set up a new committee, but the only basis is a page title, not found. Records of price concessions and new arrangements with the government were also not found. Gross margin falling below 70% in any quarter before 2027-08-31, about 40% (inferred), is the main test. The events are not mutually exclusive.

4. Open source and the two labs: who drove down whose prices

This report's view: that Nvidia supports open models is established, but the known pledged investment dollars are all on the closed-source side (amounts on the open side not found); the usage share of Chinese open-weight models on developer platforms has surged, yet the revenue and enterprise share of the two closed-source labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) have risen at the same time, so the fatal and severe-injury tiers are not supported and the reading points the other way, and the price-pressure tier is not found. These three tiers are the reading as of 2026-11-19, a starting point, not a verdict on the future. On the mechanism (inferred): in the four-link chain of Proposition C, the links of a wide price gap and rising usage share have passed; the links of same-tier or good-enough capability (public index gap of 19 points, [55] calculated) and falling share or revenue for the two (enterprise application programming interface (API) spending share of 86%, [65] confirmed) have not. The fork lies in these two links and turns on two things: whether the capability gap narrows to good enough for enterprises; and whether enterprise money follows usage -- open-source models now take 19% of enterprise tokens but only 4% of enterprise API spending ([66] confirmed). Mechanisms pointing in opposite directions are running at the same time: open substitutes eat low-end usage while closed models hold the high end and enterprise dollars; price cuts also bring more usage, so a flagship price cut is not in itself a revenue decline. Readings that would show it is happening: E8 (either lab's revenue turning negative year on year) being met; E6 (a flagship price cut of >= 20%), when met, must be read together with the two labs' revenue readings; a narrowing public index gap, a rising open-source share of enterprise API spending, and a falling combined share of enterprise API spending for the two labs.

The complements mechanism (inferred): the complement of chips is models; the more the model layer looks like a commodity, the more value stays in chips, and the more dispersed the buyers. The two labs are exactly the buyers that purchase in concentration and turn to other chips: Anthropic can obtain up to 1 million Google TPUs ([26] confirmed), and of its named compute contracts of about $643.8 billion, TPUs account for about $282.5 billion and Nvidia GPUs about $251.3 billion ([70] reported; conflicting measures not checked). So backing open models aligns with Nvidia's interest; this is a mechanism inference, not evidence of motive.

B1, his words and the releases: established. Open weights means a model's parameters are public and can be downloaded and run by oneself. The company's actions are confirmed: it released open-weight Nemotron models on 2026-06-04 and 2026-10-15 [35][37], and hosted DeepSeek-R1 on 2025-01-30 [49]. His own words are reported: on 2026-07-22 he said "These Chinese models are excellent" [34], and on 2026-01-05 he said open models "really revolutionized artificial intelligence" [33]. After his departure, Nvidia still released Nemotron for Science on 2026-11-16 [36], and the interim chief executive said support for the open-model ecosystem continues ([50] reported).

B2, supporting open more than closed: not supported (by pledged amounts). To closed-source labs: up to $100 billion for OpenAI ([42] confirmed, non-binding; he later called it "never a commitment", [43] reported) and up to $10 billion for Anthropic ([44] confirmed); amounts for the open-model companies Mistral and Reflection AI not found [38][39]; amounts actually paid on either side not found [43][44]. He has also said the world needs both open and closed models ([32], reported; a summary, not verbatim).

B3, the effect: doubtful. A token is the unit in which models measure text processed. The share of Chinese open-weight models among classifiable tokens of the top 50 on OpenRouter (a model routing platform used by developers) rose from 15.6% in 2025-11 to 72.0% in 2026-10 ([52][51] reported). The enterprise side differs: open-source models' share of enterprise tokens rose from 11% to 19%, while their share of enterprise API spending fell from 6% to 4% ([66] confirmed). A capability gap remains: on a public index the top open-weight model scores 46 and the top closed-source model 65 ([53][54] reported), a gap of 19 points ([55] calculated). The rise in share can be explained jointly by low prices, capability gains and his support; attributing it to him is capped at doubtful.

5. The espionage allegation and the procedural chain: what the absence of an indictment indicates

This report's view: the pretext proposition (that the espionage allegation was only a pretext, not the decisive reason for Jensen Huang's departure) stays at doubtful, and as of 2026-11-19 neither the upgrade nor the downgrade conditions have been triggered. The absence of an indictment carries almost no evidential weight for it, and the absence of an indictment does not mean an absence of evidence. As for whether the allegation itself is true, the content of the intelligence assessment cannot be seen through public channels, and this report does not judge it; a true allegation could also be used as the reason for his departure, so the absence of an indictment is only weakly related to whether the allegation is true.

China's motive (inferred): getting close to the chief executive of the world's most critical chip company is valuable to any intelligence agency, so the prior that he would be targeted is not low; this neither supports nor refutes the pretext proposition.

Why the downgrade rule is asymmetric: the pretext proposition rests on the premise that the allegation has no public evidence. Conversely, no indictment during the observation period would occur both if it is a pretext and if it is not, and a benchmark for the time from disclosure or referral to indictment in similar cases was not found [83], so it barely moves the judgment.
