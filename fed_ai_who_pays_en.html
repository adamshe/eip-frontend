Who pays for the AI arms race: the Fed's preparations and a 2027 to 2031 projection

AI capital spending is paid mainly out of operating cash flow; the headroom is used up and the gap is filled by borrowing, while the wages saved through layoffs cover only a small part. The Fed is assessing AI scenarios but has not yet prepared a backstop for a bubble burst, and the rates market is pricing further hikes. The weakest link is the financing chain; the chance that it breaks by the end of 2028 is about four in ten (inferred).

1. Conclusion

The speech is real: the Chair did lay out three scenarios. The three scenarios are illustrations the staff produced with models, not forecasts. The claim that the speech put special emphasis on AI disappointing does not hold on the text of the remarks. The claim that the Fed has prepared a backstop for a bubble burst has no reading to support it so far; the staff contingency plans and writing it into the response rule both reach only doubtful, and the proposition is capped at doubtful (capped at doubtful means it can be judged doubtful at most). The rates market is pricing further hikes: by 2027-12 about 64 to 89 basis points above the current range [42], not a backstop. On the who-pays chain (the chain of where the money for AI spending comes from and which hands it passes through), the weakest link is financing: the headroom in operating cash flow is used up, and the first to loosen is CoreWeave, then Oracle.

Next, the five propositions this report tests (the first contains two questions, cash flow and layoffs). That cash flow is all going into chips and servers has readings to support it: for the five companies (the four big clouds Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta, plus Oracle), capex over the last four quarters was 95.5% of operating cash flow, and in the latest quarter it exceeded it [175]; the gap is filled with debt. That layoffs are the main source of money is falsified for the three that can be calculated: on the latest round of layoffs, the wages saved are at most 3.0% of capex (calculated, [178][182][174]); on a rough count of all rounds, about 4.3% for Meta and about 3.1% for Microsoft (estimate, [176][178][180][182][174]), all below the 10% line. Unemployment and productivity currently move in opposite directions: aggregate unemployment has not risen so far, the unemployment rate is 4.1% [218], and productivity is rising; the change on the labor side is in the price channel: real hourly earnings are negative year over year (oil prices can also explain this), and the labor share has been falling since the end of 2019. Zombie firms (companies whose profits have long fallen short of their interest bills and that survive by borrowing new to repay old) cannot be seen: their share was not found, and bankruptcies are at the highest for the same period since 2010. Two-lab winner-take-all reaches only doubtful: concentration of share is reported, yet flagship price lists have only gone down, never up. Inflation and oil split into two halves: war pushing up oil and oil pushing up headline inflation has readings; oil dragging up everything else runs the other way.

In 2027, watch first the four big clouds' full-year capex guidance. Guidance turning down is one of the three conditions for the event that the financing chain breaks; the other two are credit deterioration and a deep stock market fall.

2028 is the first settlement: the financing chain breaking (two of the three: guidance turning down, credit deterioration, deep stock market fall) by 2028-12-31 is 42% (inferred); whether inflation returns to target depends on whether oil falls back to pre-war levels.

2029 to 2031 are all capped at inferred: distribution tilting toward capital has readings, attributing it to AI reaches only doubtful, and the labor share keeps falling by 2029-03-31 at 50%; the financing chain breaking by 2030-12-31 at 66%; unemployment and productivity rising together by 2030-12-31 at 15% (all inferred). The events are not mutually exclusive and do not add up.

2. What the speech said, what the Fed will do

This report's view: that the Fed is assessing three AI scenarios rests on the original text; that the Fed has prepared a backstop for an AI bubble burst (Proposition One below) has no readings yet and is capped at doubtful; the weakest link is writing it into the reaction function (the rule by which the Fed sets rates based on readings such as inflation, employment and asset prices). On the morning of 2026-11-16 Pacific time, Chair Warsh gave opening remarks at the Productivity and Jobs Task Force conference co-hosted by the Board of Governors and the San Francisco Fed ([1][2][6], confirmed), listing three paths in turn: labor and capital share the gains, major dislocation in the labor market, and "Or SI could disappoint." ([7], confirmed; SI means superintelligence). The three passages are 44, 54 and 45 words long ([11], calculated), and the third was not given special emphasis; after the three passages the Chair called himself "not exactly impartial about American leadership", and closed by naming the positive scenario as the goal ([11][9], confirmed). The remarks call these "illustrative scenarios, not forecasts" generated by staff with stylized models ([12], confirmed).

This report's measurement basis is as follows.

  • Measure: mainly US dollars; capex, operating cash flow, buybacks, debt issuance and off-balance-sheet financing, equity and the wages saved through layoffs are all counted as dollar amounts, with the ratio to capex stated; on the Fed side, count the number and content of AI-specific outputs; on the labor side, look at productivity (output divided by hours), with the unemployment rate only as a comparison; prices are split into three buckets, physical, services and assets; profits look at the shares taken by the model layer, the bottleneck layer and customers (set by this report).

Of the four readings, the one consistent with the data is routine prudence (assessing all kinds of scenarios as a matter of practice, signalling no action): the remarks call themselves illustrative scenarios ([12], confirmed), and the third was not emphasized ([11], calculated). The backstop manual (cut rates quickly if asset prices break) has no supporting reading: it is conditional, and there is no measurable reading since the speech; the 09-16 hike and core personal consumption expenditures prices at 3.1% year over year ([39][103], confirmed) only show that the current constraint is inflation, and the futures curve is also pricing hikes ([42], calculated). Verbal cooling (naming valuations without moving rates) partly fits: the 11-13 Financial Stability Report attributed part of the elevated valuations to "market enthusiasm for SI" ([31], confirmed), while the Chair used no valuation wording at the press conference ([25], confirmed). Pricking (raising rates before asset prices have fallen far, or issuing regulatory measures aimed at AI lending) has no reading since the speech; on the day of the 09-16 hike the S&P 500 drawdown was 3.17% ([110], calculated), which came before the speech, cannot test the reaction function after the speech, and counts only as the event starting point in Section 7. Precedent: after the irrational exuberance remark on 1996-12-05 came a hike on 1997-03-25, three straight cuts in autumn 1998, and an intermeeting cut on 2001-01-03 ([45][46][47][48], confirmed); comparable cases number fewer than 10, which supports only doubtful.

This report's view: by 2026-11-18 only one of the six players' falsifying readings has been triggered -- among the four big clouds (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta), Amazon's operating cash flow over the last four quarters, USD 168,300 million, is below its capex of 215,192 ([174], calculated), so the first layer to loosen is the neoclouds, the labs and Amazon, the three watched side by side; the other five players are not falsified or their reading dates have not arrived. Combined projection (inferred): before the 2028-11-07 election [240] neither the Fed nor the White House wants the financing chain to break, but per E1 in Section 7, the financing chain breaking by 2028-12-31 is 42%; by the end of 2028 the financing chain holds with about six in ten (1 - 42%) and breaks with about four in ten; by 2030-12-31 it is 66%, the two years after the election adding only about 24 more points (about 41% conditional on no break in the first two years), so the risk in the two windows is comparable. 6. Productivity and labor: does the Solow paradox still hold

This report's view: the Solow paradox still holds, judged doubtful. Labor productivity has accelerated, but the acceleration has not shown up to the same degree in total factor productivity (output growth after subtracting capital and labor inputs); aggregate unemployment has not risen so far, and the losses on the labor side show up in real wages and the labor share. Solow's original line of 1987-07-12: you can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics (reported, [101]). Overturning it takes two things at once: average annual labor productivity growth of >= 2.3% since 2019Q4, and total factor productivity above 2007-2019 on both measures; the current value is 2.223%, 0.08 percentage points short (calculated, [82]), with one measure higher and one lower [83][86].
