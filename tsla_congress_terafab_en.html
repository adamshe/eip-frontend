Tesla: Congress's Punishment Paths, the Dollar Impact, and Terafab's Odds

This report judges that through 2028-12-31 congressional Democrats can investigate on their own but not legislate (inferred); the measurable loss reaching the income statement is small, but the effect passed through valuation and the discount rate is not quantified here; Terafab's chance of on-time volume production by 2029-12-31 is about 5% (inferred), and the most likely outcome is a downgrade (55%, inferred).

1. Conclusion

This report's judgment (inferred): through 2028-12-31, what congressional Democrats can do to Tesla on their own is investigate, not legislate, and the measurable loss that reaches the income statement is small; Terafab's chance of meeting the target by 2029-12-31 is low, and by 2032-12-31 the more likely outcome is a downgrade.

The only open routes are evidence-gathering and hearings. The probability of a named hearing or formal document request by 2027-12-31 is about 85% (inferred); a subpoena by 2028-12-31 is less likely than a hearing (inferred). The probability that any negative provision becomes law by 2028-12-31, all provisions combined, is no more than 15% (inferred): the sitting president's term runs to 2029-01-20 (calculated), every provision needs his signature, and the votes to override a veto are not there (seat counts are reported).

These routes put little money through the income statement. The probability that fines and penalties cause a measurable loss of USD 1 billion or more in Tesla's income statement by 2028-12-31 is no more than 10% (inferred). That figure counts only the two routes of fines and penalties: sales, affiliate contracts, and new actions by courts and states have no countable amount (not found by this report), and value changes in the SpaceX stake Tesla holds are also outside it, so the "little money" judgment only reaches doubtful. The judgment holds only at the income-statement layer: the effect passed through valuation and the discount rate is not quantified by this report (dated segment valuations and option implied volatility were both not found by this report), so it lies outside the judgment, and a delay in self-driving approval hits what is the largest share of the valuation (inferred).

Terafab's weakest link is construction and ramp-up. The company says volume production starts in the second half of 2028 (reported); this report judges that doubtful. The probability that by 2029-12-31 it runs, in its own fab, a 3 nm-or-below process at no fewer than 10,000 wafer starts a month is about 5% (inferred); reaching that standard in 2030 to 2032 is about 25% (inferred, by 2032-12-31). The first-phase investment (reported) fits within Tesla's cash; who pays for the larger scale was not found by this report.

On price: at the 2026-11-12 close, Tesla's share price has lagged the Nasdaq 100 index fund by 0.69 percentage points since election day (calculated). The income-statement judgment and the price point the same way; this report sees no identifiable gap between price and judgment, judging by relative performance, no discount for the low-probability bad outcomes is visible either; Terafab's pricing cannot be measured. Option-market and similar pricing evidence was not found by this report, so the judgment on the price side only reaches doubtful. The two matters first meet on 2026-12-31, the start-of-construction deadline for the tax credit.

Probabilities are this report's inferences, with no model or sample behind them; data run through 2026-11-12. Sources for factual numbers are in the source table at the end; items marked inferred are this report's judgments, and items marked calculated are computed by this report from sourced numbers.

2. Premises and Timetable

Through 2029-01-20, any measure that must be written into law needs the sitting president's signature, and by party-line votes congressional Democrats cannot override a veto (calculated). The basis follows.

Both chambers' compositions are reported and not officially confirmed. House: as of 2026-11-12, media report that Democrats have won control, with 11 seats still undecided; an earlier 270toWin tally shows 254 Democratic seats, 162 Republican, and 19 undecided (reported, [1]; this report read only the page summary and did not check the underlying table). The vote gaps below are computed from these reported seats; final seats may differ. Senate: before the election Republicans held 53 seats; media say Democrats gained a net 4, at least 51 to 49 (reported, [2], 2026-11-04); the counts for the Alaska and Mississippi seats are not final (reported, [3][4]).

By default the new Congress is sworn in at noon on 2027-01-03 (confirmed, [5]). The sitting president's term runs to noon on 2029-01-20 (calculated, [6]). Overriding a veto takes two thirds of each chamber (confirmed, [7]): 67 votes in the Senate, where Democrats are 16 short; 290 in the House, where even if Democrats win every undecided seat they reach only 273 (calculated, [1][2]). Ending debate in the Senate takes 60 votes (confirmed, [8], citing an older edition of the manual), 9 short.

Bills that must pass: continuing-resolution funding runs to 2026-12-11 (confirmed, [9]), so it expires within this Congress; the FY2027 defense authorization act had not become law as of 2026-09-01 (reported, [10]); the debt-limit default date is estimated between 2027-11 and 2028-02 (estimated, [11]); the surface transportation authorization is extended to December 2026 (reported, [12]).

Naming: TERAFACTORY has no official definition; this report uses Terafab for the chip-manufacturing project Tesla and SpaceX are pursuing together (the partnership is confirmed, [13]; the 2026-03-21 launch is reported, [14]).

Any one of the negative provisions (the rider in R6, the provisions in R7, R9, and R10 that work against Tesla or its affiliates) becoming law by 2028-12-31 totals no more than 15% (inferred). The rows are not independent events: each must pass the same gate, either the president's signature or a two-thirds override in both chambers. The row probabilities in the table (R6 at 12%, R10 at 8%, R7 and R9 at 4% each) are figures taken one at a time and cannot be added; the total is capped by that gate. Basis for the cap: the override is 16 votes short in the Senate and 17 in the House (calculated, see Section 2); the sitting administration's stance toward congressional investigations was not found by this report, so the total is also not written as 0.

R3's leverage is pre-emption, not the exemption cap. Tesla has not applied for an exemption for the steering-wheel-free Cybercab and relies on self-certification (reported, [41]); the exemption cap of 2,500 vehicles per manufacturer per 12 months (confirmed, [42]) is not currently binding, and the constraint lies in NHTSA's certification audit AQ26002 and in revising the safety standards (reported, [37]; confirmed, [21]). Only if the audit rejects self-certification does the cap become binding (inferred). The 47 seats Democrats held before the election are already enough for 41 votes (calculated, [2]), so this route does not change with the election result.

Motive inference. Three hypotheses follow, each set against the facts that cut against it; all conclusions are inferred.

Hypothesis 1: the payoff is a 2028 narrative and evidence-gathering is cheap; another version says the payoff is deterring large donors. Support: from 2025-01-01 to 2026-09-30 the subject made 128 personal donations totaling USD 369 million (confirmed, [43]). Counter-examples: (1) as of 2026-11-12 this report found no written agenda from Democratic leaders or incoming chairs naming Tesla or its affiliates; (2) the subject announced on 2025-05-28 that he was leaving government (reported, [33]), and oversight of the sitting administration will compete for committee time; (3) the party affiliation of the recipients was not checked by this report [43]. Conclusion: the donor-deterrence layer of the motive depends on the recipients' party affiliation, and this report does not adopt it. Hypothesis 2: the cost falls on Tesla's US plants and employees, EV policy moves in the same direction as Tesla's sales, so EV economics will not be touched. Counter-examples: (1) the bill the House passed in 2021 added USD 4,500 for union-assembled vehicles (reported, [26]), and Tesla was not among them (inferred); (2) the California DMV ruled on 2025-12-16 that Tesla violated state law (confirmed, [39]), so a pro-EV state penalizes Tesla all the same; (3) the number of US employees was not found by this report [44], so the cost item cannot be measured. This report's judgment: someone will propose a credit design adverse to Tesla, and it will not get past the signature.

Hypothesis 3: the aim is to probe DOGE, tighten affiliate contracts, and slow self-driving. Counter-examples: (1) the R3 paragraph above: the exemption cap is not the constraint; (2) tightening contract terms must be written into law, writing into law needs a signature, and provisions covering only sitting officials do not reach someone who has left [33]; (3) the only reference for a congressional investigation carrying through to a company's dollar loss is the Boeing case, which was followed by legislation (confirmed, [18]); cases where the executive branch did not cooperate were not found by this report [18]. This report's judgment: the aim is evidence-gathering and hearings plus no federal pre-emption; the contract-terms item is low probability.

Ladder: letter -> hearing -> subpoena -> contempt -> passage in one chamber -> law. The letter step has already been taken (confirmed, [22][23]). Counting from 2027-01-03, an 18-month investigation reports around 2028-07 (calculated, [18]); a subpoena that is resisted goes to civil litigation, and the precedent ran about 21 months from suit to settlement (reported, [16], dates incomplete); the precedent of a suit 22 days after a contempt referral came when the Justice Department cooperated (reported, [16]). This report judges that by 2028-12-31 the investigation line reaches a subpoena with probability 60% and a contempt vote with 20%, and most likely stops at the subpoena step; on the legislation line, passage in one chamber is 55%, and any negative provision becoming law, in total, is no more than 15%.

6. Scaling Terafab Up, and the Four Tiers of Probability

Extended to the scale the company describes, the money for the first phase does not run out; what runs out is time (Section 5, Link 5). At the hundred-billion-dollar scale, whose cash flow breaks first depends on the split, and the split was not found by this report; only if Tesla's share exceeds about USD 45 billion does it exceed its cash and short-term investments (calculated, [48]).

The scale-up leans on the dollar axis. The fab-side axis cannot be done: monthly wafer starts, EUV exposure layers per wafer, throughput per tool, investment per unit of capacity, and TSMC's total capacity were all not found by this report [70][79][103]. The figure of 1 terawatt of compute a year rests on a media relay only ([71] reported), and serves as a single comparison: it equals 31% (calculated) of the roughly 3.2 TW of average global electric power in 2025 ([104] calculated); no judgment rests on it.

Dollar axis: investment grows linearly with capacity, and Tesla's free cash flow does not grow with it. The first phase is about USD 16.8 billion ([61] reported, 2026-08-06), and Tesla's share was not found by this report ([13]). Taking two hypothetical cases: paying half is USD 8.4 billion and paying all is USD 16.8 billion, which are 2.5x and 5.1x trailing four-quarter (2025 Q4 to 2026 Q3) free cash flow of USD 3.30 billion (calculated, [47]), and 18% and 37% of cash and short-term investments of USD 45.447 billion ([48] confirmed, 2026-09-30) (calculated). The company's stated long-term figure of more than USD 100 billion ([84] reported, 2026-10-21): if Tesla paid half, that would exceed all of its cash and short-term investments, and in that case the cost does not hold (estimated), with a re-check date of 2027-01 (the 2026 annual report). Opposite branch: media say SpaceX may put in up to USD 119 billion ([61] reported; this report did not find this figure in SpaceX's filing); if true, the main payer is not Tesla.
