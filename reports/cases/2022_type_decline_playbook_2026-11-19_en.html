If 2027 Turns Into 2022: Timing, Depth and a Collar Battle Plan

1. Conclusion

This report's view: if the decline after mid-January 2027 takes the shape of 2022, what matters most is not the first decline but the second decline after the first rebound; the core of the plan is to put on full-position protection when the rebound stalls, not to buy the full protection during the first decline. Run against the 2022 price path, this staged collar (hold the stock + buy puts + sell calls) narrows the full-year loss from -59% for the stock alone to about -9%, and the maximum drawdown from -71% to about -37%. These numbers rest on one premise: the exit rule was revised once based on this year's path. The original rule was to exit when NET itself regained its January high; under it the result is -52%, because in March 2022 NET's rebound went above its January high, so the protection was removed before the biggest decline; only after switching to watching the broad market alone is the result -9%. If the scenario does not come, the first scout put costs about 2% to 3% of position value for nothing.

2. Assumptions and Premises

This is a scenario, not a forecast. The scenario: after mid-January 2027, US stocks go through a 2022-style decline in which rising rates crush valuations (earnings hold up, valuations are compressed).

Why only one sample, 2022: cycles like this come only once every few years; against a backdrop of high rates plus an AI investment wave, the only complete bear market available for comparison is 2022. With only one sample, the dates and magnitudes in this report are "windows laid out on the shape of 2022", not statistical regularities; the first version of the plan will be revised after its first contact with the market, and the falsification conditions below are prepared for that moment.

4. Depth: Two Ways to Size the Broad Market, NET Back-Solved from Its Multiple

There are two ways to size the broad market. Copy the decline: the Nasdaq 100 falls 36%. Copy the valuation floor: in 2022 the S&P 500's forward price-to-earnings ratio was compressed from about 21.5x to 15.3x (estimate, not checked against primary data); it is about 17.2x now, and compressing it to the same 15.3x is a decline of about 11%; if earnings expectations are also cut 5%, the decline is about 15%, which corresponds to a Nasdaq 100 decline of about 20% (estimate). This report's view leans toward the latter: the earnings base this time is thicker than in 2022, and the valuation starting point is lower than in 2022.

NET's multiple to the Nasdaq 100: 1.50 since its listing, 2.21 in the 2022 bear market, 1.14 over the last 250 trading days (calculated from daily closes [1]). Four rules:

  1. Buy puts early and small: buy only a half-position scout put before the first decline; buy the full-position main-course put only when the rebound stalls.
  2. Sell calls after confirmation: sell calls only after the decline is confirmed; sell none during the rebounds.
  3. Close at the bottom: at the bottom of each leg, sell the puts; buy back the calls once they fall below 20% of the sale price, without waiting for expiry.
  4. Fixed-ratio strikes: put at price -15%, call at price +20%; do not move them toward at-the-money.

Falsification and termination:

  • Falsification: after the decline is confirmed, any day the Nasdaq 100 closes above its January high = the scenario is wrong; close everything. Watch only the broad market, not NET itself: in March 2022 NET rebounded 41% and at one point closed above its January high, while the broad market was still about 8% below its high, and NET then fell another 69%.
  • Termination: if by 2027-03-22 the Nasdaq 100 has never closed 8% below its January high, sell the scout put, sell no calls, and end the plan.

Run against the 2022 price path (from 2022-01-10 to year-end, options priced at the actual volatility of the time, with sensitivity at 20% higher and 20% lower): stock only -59.2%; buy puts only, no calls sold -31% (-26% to -36%); full plan -9.4% (-12% to -7%). If NET fell only three-quarters or half as much as in 2022 (stock only -49% or -36%), the full plan is about -13% or -15%. None of the three calls had its strike crossed. If the scenario does not come: the scout put costs about 2% to 3% net for nothing; the worst false alarm (falsified only after the main course is on) costs about 6% to 7%.

6. The Reason Behind Each Rule

Why the first decline is light and the second heavy. All three reasons come from the 2022 path. First, when the first decline happens it cannot be told whether it is an ordinary pullback or the start of a 2022-type decline; it is itself the confirmation signal, so only half a position goes on. Second, the first rebound carried NET back near its starting point (at the end of March 2022 it briefly closed above its early-January price), and buying protection there gives the highest strikes. Third, the second decline is the largest (NET -69.5% versus -27.0% in the first decline). So the full-position protection goes on the second decline.

Why no call during the rebounds. Bear-market rebounds are fast and violent; NET's two rebounds in 2022 were +41% and +96%. Calls sold then are the most likely to be crossed, and selling them trades the upside for small money. Sell none during the rebound; wait until it stalls (the close starts falling below the high two bars earlier) and sell then, after the rebound has used up its strength.

Why buy puts early and sell calls after confirmation. A put is insurance; after a decline is confirmed, implied volatility spikes first and the insurance becomes much more expensive, so buy half a position first, while things are calm. A call is income; after a decline is confirmed, implied volatility is high and the same strike brings in more money, so wait.

Why not buy now. Right now is just after a sharp drop, with implied volatility around 66% to 70%; if it falls back to 50%, puts get about 30% to 40% cheaper and calls about 40% to 50% cheaper [2]. Also, the scenario before mid-January 2027 is a rise first, so insurance bought now has to sit through a rally first, and its time value drains away for nothing.

7. Limits, and What Shows the Scenario Has Not Come

Limits: there is only one sample, 2022, and the date windows and magnitude thresholds both come from that same year, so the backtest results are naturally flattering; every number in this report only shows "it holds on this one 2022 path", not a rule. The bottom timing and magnitude thresholds did not catch the low in 2022 either (under the rule, the second-decline puts were closed on June 9, and NET then fell another 23% before bottoming). The backtest has no option quotes from that year and prices options with historical volatility, which overstates far-dated option prices after a sharp drop. In mid-January 2022 NET was already 54% below its November 2021 high, while the 2027 scenario has the top only in mid-January, so the first decline is not fully comparable. This report's basis:

Measure: price, and option mid price as a share of the current price Start: 2027-01-15 (scenario) and 2022-01-10 (backtest) Driver: rising rates crushing valuations Benchmark: stock only

What shows the scenario has not come: after mid-January the Nasdaq 100 never closes 5% below its year-to-date high; or by March 22 it has never been 8% below; or after the decline is confirmed the Nasdaq 100 regains its January high. If any one of these appears, the plan is closed out under the rules above.
