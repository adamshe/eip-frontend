Conclusion: a financial shock first, a god kept in the lab after

The coming decade will not open with an elegant technological singularity. The likelier script: a financial shock from a cliff in AI investment arrives first; America slides into recession and AI stocks fall hard; artificial general intelligence (AGI: AI that can do most knowledge work) quietly crosses the threshold amid the market's wreckage; and the state, neither tightening its belt nor defaulting, thins the bill out slowly with mild inflation and targeted handouts. In the end the money flows toward the things that cannot be copied. Of the two possible orders, crisis first or intelligence first, we give the first 65% odds. That number should be read in two halves. A credit event in the AI chain (a major data-center landlord defaulting, swapping its debt at a discount, or being forced to sell) before AGI: about 65%. An actual American recession, also before AGI: only about 50 to 55%. The gap of ten-odd points between them is a third road, in which credit breaks but the economy only slows and never falls into recession. The market is already testing the credit half for us; the recession half is the step in the whole chain with the thinnest evidence. Both numbers are judgments, not computations, and both are bets against the market's prevailing expectation (see the Epilogue).

When each link should show: a timetable for the conclusion

The chain will play out on the calendar: credit answers first, recession follows a beat later, AGI rises from the wreckage, and the state and the money show their hand last. The credit half has already begun: this October, neocloud stocks and bonds are already being repriced. The hard dates ahead are CoreWeave's first monthly debt-service test around November 30 (CoreWeave is the largest data-center landlord), the first test on another of its loans on February 28, 2027, and the maturity of Nebius's bridge loan on October 16, 2027. One default, discounted debt swap or forced sale in that tier settles it; even a 2028 event still comes before AGI.

Recession is the link most likely to fail, and comes in the second half of 2027 at the earliest. A credit break takes several quarters to reach wages and output. Borrowing only the lag from 2007: the subprime lenders fell in spring, and the recession arrived at year end. So watch the second half of 2027 through 2028 for two quarters of contraction, with unemployment climbing from 4.2% toward 5%; AI stocks start falling hard in autumn 2027 and bottom in the second half of 2028. If credit breaks but the economy is still growing in autumn 2028, that is the third road, like the last shale-oil default wave, which passed without an American recession.

Intelligence does not wait for credit; the median stays at the fourth quarter of 2028. If before the end of 2027 a lab finds that AI can do AI research on its own, intelligence-first gains ten points and AGI could come as early as 2027; on that road the bubble inflates into 2028 and bursts in 2029 to 2031. The state comes last: the election on November 7, 2028; failed austerity in 2029; then in 2030 and 2031 a 10-year yield held below inflation, when money visibly flows toward what cannot be copied.

Prologue: two popular misreadings

Misreading one: "The AI bubble will detonate America's debt and bring down the empire." It will not. America's nominal economy is growing at about 6.3% a year, and the deficit runs at about 6.2% of gross domestic product (GDP); the two roughly cancel out. Because the federal debt held by the public is almost exactly one year of GDP, the debt burden rises only about one percentage point a year. Debt is an amplifier, not a detonator. The real detonator is buried elsewhere: a cliff in AI capital spending, and the local credit defaults it sets off.

Misreading two: "When AGI arrives, everything deflates and cash is king." That is only half right. AI really will make cheaper everything that can be reproduced by code and assembly line. But the other half is just as real: land, licenses, grid connections, wafer capacity, scarce compute, the things that cannot be copied, will grow ever more expensive in a world that keeps printing money. The future is not a one-way deflation but a pair of scissors: what can be copied gets cheaper, what cannot gets dearer, and the currency itself is slowly diluted. Today the Fed is still raising rates and inflation stands at 3.7%; one blade of the scissors is already in plain sight.

Act One: the cliff and the clearing (2026-2028)

Technology never advances in a vacuum. It runs on bills that someone in the real world can actually pay, one after another.

Today, capability is rising and the bills are stretched tight. The six main builders of AI have nearly doubled their capital spending this year. Wall Street expects another 30 to 50 percent next year, but growth at that pace can only be sustained by ever-larger increments each year: even if every builder simply holds this year's second-half pace without adding more, next year's total rises only about 14%. We are betting that growth drops, because of two constraints: the power cannot be connected, and the money increasingly has to be borrowed. The leading labs look like the masters of a new age, but financially they behave more like tenants. Take Anthropic: it reportedly carries hundreds of billions of dollars of cloud-compute contracts, most of them non-cancelable; the data centers are not its own, and nearly half of its revenue flows in through the channels of two clouds, Amazon and Google. Follow the money and the labs' coming IPOs look like a handoff: while liquidity is still plentiful, the risk passes to later buyers. The market has already caught the scent. Stocks along the AI chain are down anywhere from 12% to 51% from their highs; Oracle and CoreWeave, the heaviest borrowers, have lost 60 to 70 percent of their value; and CoreWeave's bonds are reportedly priced as distressed debt.

The break will start at the weakest link. In fact credit has already moved first: this October, neocloud stocks and bonds are being repriced, and the slice of investment that builds data centers with borrowed money will be the first to be choked. From now through the first half of 2027 is a selling season: labs going public, giants issuing new shares, private AI assets packed into the indexes. In the second half of 2027 the first guidance of "flat spending next year" will appear, and the most indebted layer will give way first: CoreWeave, which rents out AI data centers; Oracle, which borrows to build them; and OpenAI, which pays for its spending with round after round of fundraising. Once investment turns from slowing to shrinking, recession follows. Tax receipts fall, relief spending climbs, the deficit swells to 8 to 10 percent of GDP, and the Fed turns around to cut rates and buy bonds.

AGI will raise its head in the trough. A capital winter cannot stop contracts already signed or models already in training: the cash raised in the IPOs keeps the compute bills paid, and the next generation of models keeps training. The likeliest timing is around the fourth quarter of 2028, near the market's bottom. The crisis does not stop AGI. It does something else: it pushes control of AGI out of the hands of risk capital and toward the state and a few giants.

The other script diverges in just one place. If, in early 2027, the four big tech companies' spending plans keep climbing sharply and lab revenue keeps racing ahead, the order flips. The AGI story first inflates the bubble to its peak, and the bubble bursts in 2029 to 2031, from a higher start and with a deeper fall. The destination is the same; only the order differs.

Act Two: the state quietly thins the money (2029-2032)

In the 2028 election, "AI is taking our jobs" will be the top issue. The new administration will try to tighten the belt in 2029 and fail in the recession's aftermath. What follows becomes the new normal: an undeclared financial repression, in which long-term rates are held below inflation, real rates turn negative, and debt is dissolved by time. Two markers will be visible: the 10-year Treasury yield staying below inflation, and banks being allowed, or steered, to hold more Treasuries.

Nor will distribution be a romantic "money for everyone." The state will first hand out goods and services: housing vouchers, health care, dedicated accounts. Then comes a targeted transition income for the white-collar workers whom AI pushes out. A true universal basic income remains only a tail scenario; were it ever legislated, the gap could be filled only by printing money. Cities that live on Wall Street bonuses will feel the pain earlier: more than a year after their revenue caves in, the odds of an across-the-board property tax rate increase rise above one half.

By 2029 to 2030 the physical bottlenecks, gas turbines and grid connections, begin to ease, and the market reconcentrates among the survivors. That is where recovery begins.

Act Three: who wins, who loses

The decade's transfer of wealth runs along a very plain line: the winners hold the bottlenecks, hold the entrances, or issue the licenses; the losers are leveraged tenants and people who live on wages alone.

  • Those who hold the bottlenecks: the wafer foundries, and the cloud giants sitting on vast compute. The profits of chip design can change hands, but every chip still has to pass through a wafer fab.
  • Those who hold the entrances: Google already owns the default assistant slot on Android and supplies Apple's underlying model. Search advertising is not dead; what is fading is the business of selling ads on other people's websites.
  • Those who issue the licenses: governments. They will not take over the labs, but they decide who may use the strongest models and when those models go live. For the digital dollar, the state has chosen regulated stablecoins, not bitcoin.

In a world of negative real rates, land, equity in a machine economy that keeps throwing off cash, and long-term fixed-rate debt all stand on the winning side. The losers are just as clear: tenants who borrowed to chase AI; passive funds and retail investors who bought new issues at the top; people who hold cash and Treasuries for the long run (cash will briefly come out ahead in the crisis, then be slowly diluted); asset-light companies built on "software scarcity"; and white-collar workers fresh out of college. Already today, new college graduates have a harder time finding work than the workforce as a whole.

Act Four: a god kept in the lab, and breakthroughs that reprice before they deliver

Superintelligence (ASI: better than the best humans in almost every field) will not be rolled out to the public as a product. It will stay in the lab, with the state standing beside it. The shape is visible already: the strongest model has been announced but not opened to the public, and a single government export-control order took a frontier model offline worldwide for nearly three weeks. What the world looks like after superintelligence appears has no mechanism we can reason forward from, so this report gives no date and watches only for signals.

This differs at one key point from the popular story that "the state will take superintelligence under military control." The state can leash release and export, but it cannot leash self-improvement inside the lab, and it cannot come up with the annual spending a takeover would require. The likelier form: the lab holds it, and the state is in the room.

Fusion, room-temperature superconductivity, a cure for cancer and the reversal of aging: each of these four breakthroughs has to be read in two stages. At the moment of discovery, finance reprices first; whether the discovery is real can usually be verified within a month, and what moves is asset valuations, pension actuarial assumptions and the political agenda. Delivery, though, travels the slow road of physics and law. A new material typically takes a decade or more to go from discovery to large-scale use; fusion power plants arrive after 2035; new cancer therapies reach partial availability in 2030 to 2033 at the earliest; and reversing aging first requires the rules to change. So within this decade these breakthroughs change expectations, not lives; they change prices, not the order of events described above.

As for the picture of "material abundance, with the masses gently kept and fed," its weakest points lie not with the masses but in two other places: the money handed to the masses comes from printing, not from rents and taxes; and the knowledge elite pushed out of work will organize and demand redistribution. These two cracks will first show in 2028 to 2032.

Epilogue: the strongest counterargument, and what would prove us wrong

The strongest counterargument says this is a slowdown, not a cliff. The market's prevailing expectation stands on its side, and Alphabet has already said next year's spending will "increase significantly." The main spenders are the few giants with the deepest cash piles, while the risks at Oracle and CoreWeave are already mostly out in the open; and even if those two fail, the damage may look like the telecom bankruptcies of 2001, which hurt investors rather than the whole economy. Our answer has two parts, along with two concessions. First, the economy feeds on the increment. Even if every builder merely holds this year's second-half pace, investment's push to growth shrinks by more than half; to keep that push, each year must bring more new money than the year before, and these companies have already begun this year to fill the gap by issuing debt and new shares. Second, the Fed is still raising rates; the cost of money is going up, not down. The first concession: whether slower spending alone can drag America into recession is the step in the whole chain with the thinnest evidence. The second: if the counterargument is right, the result is not the other order but a third road, a slowdown without a recession and no deep fall within the decade; this edition now gives that road a probability of its own: credit breaking first is about 65%, recession arriving first about 50 to 55%, and the difference of roughly 10 to 15 points is this road. So the judgment rides on a single date. At the end of January 2027, if the four giants' spending plans rise another 30 percent or more combined and lab revenue keeps growing fast, our own rule moves the two orders to fifty-fifty.

This judgment could be overturned in five places:

  • In early 2027 the four tech giants' spending plans accelerate again and lab revenue keeps growing fast, while borrowing costs for high-risk companies stay low throughout. That is the world in which intelligence comes before the crisis.
  • OpenAI's current round finally closes at a valuation of $1.4 trillion or more, and the money does not come from those who sell it compute. It has reportedly cut the target to $1.25 trillion and asked for commitments to be locked in by October 30; if the reports are right, this counter-signal has not appeared for now.
  • A frontier lab's safety evaluation finds that AI can already do AI research and development on its own. AGI could then arrive as early as 2027.
  • The economy is still growing, yet unemployment rises above 5%. That would show joblessness following technology, and the fight over distribution would arrive two or three years early.
  • Electricity prices fall for a full year in a row. That would mean the technological deflation we reject has truly arrived.

The five dates most worth watching:

  • 2026-10-26 to 11-02 (expected): Anthropic makes its prospectus public. Watch the gross margin; how many times revenue its compute commitments amount to; whether revenue is concentrated in a few customers and in the cloud channels; and whether the governance section states who can order a shutdown.
  • 2026-10-28 to 10-30: the first word on next year's spending in the third-quarter reports of Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon, and the locking-in of commitments for OpenAI's new round (reportedly about $30 billion). If all three say next year will "increase substantially," the cliff the main line needs has to wait; if OpenAI fails to raise the full amount or its valuation falls below $1 trillion, the main line strengthens. At the end of March 2027, check again whether the money has arrived and who put it up.
  • Late December 2026: Anthropic's 30th day as a listed company. A clear break below the issue price strengthens the main line; holding above it with a still-rising valuation strengthens the other order.
  • 2027-01-27 to 02-04: the four tech giants (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta) announce their 2027 spending plans. This is the main test. Flat or down, and our confidence in the main line rises to 70%; up 30% or more combined, with lab revenue still growing fast, and the two orders are close to fifty-fifty.
  • Mid-October 2027: the full-year federal financial statement for fiscal 2027. Interest above 21% of federal revenue would show debt beginning to move from the background to the foreground, as an amplifier.

Timeline: the decade week by week, month by month

The order of events will most likely be settled within the next hundred days. The years after that will mostly be spent confirming it.

The first two months open the selling season. Anthropic's prospectus is expected to go public within the next week or two, with pricing aimed at landing before Thanksgiving. OpenAI, according to reports, has cut the valuation target for its new funding round from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion and wants the money committed by October 30. In that same week, the third-quarter reports of three tech giants will give the first hint of how they talk about next year's spending. Then, between December 8 and 18, the Fed's dot plot, the expiry of the government's stopgap funding, a large lockup expiry for SpaceX's pre-IPO shareholders, the big quarterly options expiry and index rebalancing all crowd into the same few days, and money is pulled out from several directions at once. Right behind them comes Anthropic's thirtieth day as a public company: if it trades below its issue price, the main line grows stronger; if it holds above the issue price while its valuation keeps climbing, the other ordering does.

January 27 to February 4, 2027 is the main test; the spring that follows is the peak of confirmation and of supply. The four tech giants announce their 2027 spending plans. If spending is flat or falling, our confidence in the main line rises to seventy percent; if the combined total jumps by another thirty percent or more and lab revenue keeps racing ahead, it drops to even odds. On March 31 we check whether OpenAI's round has actually arrived and who put up the money. In mid-to-late May, Anthropic's early shareholders come out of lockup; in mid-June, the large founder tranche at SpaceX unlocks, and that is the week when the first half's flood of new stock peaks.

From the second half of 2027 a monthly view is enough, and after 2029 a quarterly and half-yearly one. At the end of July, Microsoft's guidance for its next fiscal year is the most likely place for the first "flat spending next year." In late summer, Oracle's leverage crosses the line the rating agencies watch. In mid-October the federal government closes its books for the year, and the share of revenue swallowed by interest will tell us when debt steps out of the background and onto center stage. In 2028 the recession bottoms out, the seventh-generation models go live, and the country votes on November 7. Beyond that, the calendar holds Congress, the courts and a long run of maturity dates; superintelligence does not enter the timeline at all, and only its signals are listed.

Key conclusions

  • Main judgment: the crisis comes before AGI, at 65% (the earlier working assumption was 60%; naughty boss adopted 65% on 2026-10-25, and confirmed keeping it after the review found that the +14% spending figure is an assumption, not a forecast). The 65% refers to the order "crisis first" versus "intelligence first". It is a judgment, not a number computed by a model, and not the joint probability that every link in the chain happens on schedule; the 65% is further split into two numbers: a credit event before AGI about 65%, a recession before AGI about 50-55%, and the gap of about 10-15% is the third road, "credit breaks but only a slowdown, no recession." The trigger is not "the debt blows up by itself" but a chain: a cliff in AI spending growth -> the weakest credit link breaks first -> recession -> the fiscal deficit swells to 8-10% of GDP -> AI stocks fall hard -> AGI arrives near the bottom. Three things stand on this side: the Fed was still raising rates on 2026-09-16 [verified] (though the policy rate of 3.75-4.00% is still below nominal growth of 6.3%, so the squeeze is limited); AI-chain stocks are down 12-51% from their highs, and the heaviest borrowers, Oracle and CoreWeave, are down 62-70% [verified: 2026-10-23 close], with CoreWeave's 2032 notes yielding about 15% and Oracle's five-year credit default swap (CDS) at a record 288bp [secondhand: reports of 2026-10-15]; and the median AGI date has moved back to 2028Q4 [estimate]. Two things stand on the other side: AI revenue is growing faster than assumed (2026E +250% [estimate]); and Wall Street's consensus for 2027 spending is still another 30-50% rise (about $1.1 trillion for five companies combined; Goldman Sachs has $1.2 trillion, i.e., +54% [secondhand: relayed 2026-09-28]), while Alphabet's chief financial officer has already said 2027 spending will "increase significantly" [secondhand: relayed from the 2026 first-quarter earnings call]. The "2027 spending about +14%" that this report originally wrote is only a mechanical extrapolation of "holding the 2026 second-half pace without adding more" [estimate], not a forecast; the main scenario bets that the guidance at the end of January 2027 comes in clearly below consensus, which makes it a contrarian call. The thinnest step in the chain is "slower spending is enough to pull the economy into recession"; the check date is 2026-10-29 (the components of third-quarter GDP). The first weak signal comes around late 2026-12 (30 days after Anthropic lists); the main test date is 2027-01-27..02-04 (the four giants' 2027 spending guidance); the OpenAI funding gate has moved forward to about 2026-10-30 (commitments locked in [secondhand]), and 2027-03-31 is now only a check on whether the money arrived and who supplied it.

4. Chronology 2026Q4-2036

Bottom line: The two orderings differ in only one thing: "does the deep fall in AI stocks come before AGI or after it". The endpoint is the same. The main scenario is written as crisis-first (65%). Crisis-first splits into two numbers: a credit event in the AI chain (a major neocloud defaults, does a distressed exchange or is forced to sell) before AGI, about 65%; an American recession before AGI, about 50-55%; the gap of about 10-15% = the third road where credit breaks but the economy only slows, without recession. A credit event before AGI rests mainly on the smaller neoclouds and the IPO window, not on CoreWeave itself (its main default window is around 2030-31, later than the AGI median). The earliest date to tell the two apart is 2027-02-04 (when all four giants have given guidance); the first weak signal comes around late 2026-12.

Crisis-first ordering (crisis before AGI, 65%): The cash Anthropic raises in its IPO lets it keep paying for already-signed compute through the downturn, which decouples capability progress from the 2027 credit cycle; that is why "AGI arrives at the low" holds up mechanically. Counter-evidence: if the IPO slips to 2027 or raises < $50B [estimated threshold], the timing of Gen 7 moves later with the credit cycle and the AGI median pushes out to 2029.

11. Review: premises, mismatches and weak points

Bottom line: The biggest structural problem in the original working assumptions is not any single wrong number but two kinds of conflation: treating two-point extrapolations and other people's forecasts as statistics, and treating things that happen at the same time as a definition. The single premise with the biggest effect on the conclusions is "the agents' money passes through the edge network"; the name that looks most like "using short-term heat to argue for a long-term hold" is Robinhood.

(1) Mechanistic reasoning passing itself off as statistics

  • "Each generation shrinks by about 15%": there are only two intervals, so this is a two-point extrapolation.
  • "Two generations on, the 80% time horizon reaches 1 week to 3 months": an extrapolation on a yardstick that has stopped updating and is unreliable above 16 hours.
  • The industry GW curve: the source was checked, the forecast was not; it is a statement of demand and intent, not construction statistics.
  • "One GW is about a million instances": that is arithmetic, not measurement; and price is again being treated as cost.
  • All the UBI figures, and "getting back to a 3% deficit takes a 1.2T adjustment": these are calculations and cannot stand beside the verified MTS figures as evidence.
  • "A 50-80% fall over 5-8 years": there is only one precedent, IBM.
  • The weights on the orderings (including this report's 65/35): the criteria can be measured, the weights themselves cannot.
  • "2027E spending +14%": an extrapolation of a flat pace that was once treated as a fact on the crisis-first side (corrected in the pre-publication review).
  • "Spending slowdown -> recession": no direct evidence; this is the thinnest step in the chain; check date 2026-10-29 (the contribution of equipment and structures investment among the components of third-quarter GDP).
  • The third road beyond the two orderings (credit breaks but only a slowdown, no recession, no deep fall within the decade): about 10-15%, derived as the gap between 65 and 50-55, with no independent evidence.

(2) Mismatched judgment types

  • Robinhood: it is framed with long-term-hold reasoning, but the grounds for listing it were trading and gambling heat (short-term momentum); its only long-term rationale (a state distribution pipe) appeared later.
  • "BTC surge = the game is decided": treats a price signal (timing) as an institutional conclusion (structure).
  • Zero-coupon convertibles as "buyers locking up their holdings": reads volatility arbitrage (timing) as a directional signal (structure).
  • The closing condition of the power window was written as a technical condition (efficiency): in reality it is set by funding and policy.
  • Google was tested as an "AI victim": its core assets are not built on software scarcity but on default distribution and compute.

(3) Premises we disagree with

  • BTC is the rail the state chose: we disagree. The GENIUS final rule [verified]; the draft small-transaction tax exemption covers only stablecoins [secondhand]; the reserve holds only seized coins [secondhand].
  • The cognitive wage floor tends to zero: the frontier is raising prices [verified: pricing pages], and human wages are still propped up by legal liability and physical presence.
  • The income tax base collapses first: in FY2026 the direction is the reverse [verified].
  • Continual learning is solved: there was no learning in deployment in 2026 [verified].
  • Buildings lose value in 2029-31: construction costs are still +4.46% [verified], and the construction site is the last stop.
  • Meta = a network of person-to-person relationships: friends' content is down to 17% / 7% [secondhand].
  • "Wafers and power -> 2028-29": power is slower than wafers, and the physical bottleneck eases only in 2029-30 (turbine capacity sold out through 2030 [verified], interconnection median over 5 years [verified]).

(4) Confidence: for each item see the table in Section 1. The item with the widest disagreement is the "cognitive wage floor": one analysis gave it 55, another used primary evidence from the pricing pages to show the frontier is raising prices, and the final ruling is 45.

(5) Which ordering is likelier: crisis-first, 65% (a credit event before AGI); of which a recession before AGI about 50-55%. Criteria in Section 4.

(6) Which error would cost the most: "the agents' money passes through the edge network." The main leg of the $1 trillion market-cap scenario for Cloudflare (8.6x the current price) is agent payments; if the wallet, directory and settlement go to AWS + Coinbase / Stripe (which is the shape today [verified]), Cloudflare is left with only security and CDN, its price-to-sales ratio falls from about 42x back to 15-24x, a move of -43% to -64% [estimate], and this would stack on top of the deep fall in AI stocks. Two of the four going wrong = the long-term rationale is reduced to "entrance + identity."

(7) The name that looks most like "using short-term heat to argue for a long-term hold": Robinhood. Event contracts contributed about 46% of the $319 million second-quarter revenue increase [estimate], while crypto revenue fell 38% [verified]; prediction markets have no moat (only about 25% of Kalshi volume comes from Robinhood users [secondhand]); its only long-term rationale is its trustee role for Trump Accounts [verified], whose return profile looks more like a utility pipe. (8) Outside logic we discarded, and what happens if we discarded it wrongly

  • "AI broadly surpasses almost everyone" (discarded on the ground that AI has no legal personhood): half wrong to discard. AI can be put inside a corporate shell to substitute economically; the legal layer can block signatures but not work -> the protection of license, credential and permit-type assets is weaker than assumed.

  • "Africa catches up, lifespans of 150 years, mental illness cured": conditional statements; the cost of discarding them wrongly is negligible.
  • Narratives discarded from the outside scenario framework (2026-10-25, Section 14), and what happens if we discarded them wrongly:

Question (1): does the three-layer symbiosis have a fatal internal instability?

  • Bottom line: it has two fatal points, and neither is "mass discontent": transfers are funded by printing money rather than by rents; and the knowledge elite pushed out of work will organize a politics of redistribution. Earliest 2028-2032 (recession, election and transition-insurance legislation in the same window).
  • Mechanism and evidence:
    • A distributive state is stable only if "collectible rents / beneficiaries" is large enough. The Gulf oil states rely on high rents per head and pay dividends only to a minority of citizens; Rome's grain dole relied on tribute from the provinces, not on local taxes [none re-checked]. The American equivalent: AI end revenue of about $160.3B in 2026E [estimate], spread over about 268 million adults, is about $600 a year each; a universal floor of $18,000-24,000 a year is 30-40 times that; even if AI revenue reaches 2T in 2030, a 30% tax yields about $2,200 a person, about a tenth of the floor [estimate]. The gap is filled by printing (Section 5: 0.8-2.4T a year) -> money flows to assets that cannot be copied -> "spending 2,000 like 20,000" holds only for reproducible goods, and runs the other way for rents, locations and services provided by people.
    • America's version of "provincial tribute" is dollar seigniorage: stablecoins sell dollars abroad and are a new tribute channel; today they total $302.8B, flat for a year [verified] -> this funding source has not started.
    • The other half of the Gulf model is a two-tier split of citizens and non-citizens: if America moves to a distributive regime, eligibility conditions (residence, status) will become the main political battlefield, which is the same issue as "UBI tied to residence" in Section 7 [estimate].
    • Historically, those who organized redistribution and revolution were mostly the marginalized upper strata, not masses pacified by entertainment: unemployment among recent college graduates is 5.6% versus 4.2% overall, with underemployment at 42.0% [verified: NY Fed 2026Q2]. People who are displaced have the capacity to organize, and games will not pacify them.
    • The only mechanism that would make the framework's third layer real is a government that no longer needs the masses as soldiers and taxpayers (autonomous weapons + an AI tax base). The first signal has appeared: in 2026-02 the Pentagon asked for restrictions on autonomous armed drones to be lifted and, after being refused, listed Anthropic as a supply-chain risk [secondhand: AP 2026-09-25]. This step falls in the zone where superintelligence spills out; per the ruling we record only the signal and make no point forecast.
  • Indicators and dates: California billionaire tax (2026-11-03); the gap between recent-graduate and overall unemployment (quarterly, next around 2026-11, alarm at >= 2.5pp); total stablecoins (daily; a new high > $315.5B = the tribute channel starts); both parties' 2028 platforms (around 2028-08); FY2027 interest/revenue (mid-2027-10).

Question (3): does the framework underestimate the hard time constraints of the physical world?

  • Bottom line: yes, badly. Intelligence compresses "finding the answer"; it cannot compress scaling up production, certification and standards, turnover of the capital stock, and human follow-up and approval, and these stages have historically taken up most of the time from discovery to wide use.
  • Mechanism and evidence:
    • Materials: discovery to deployment usually takes 10-20 years (the US Materials Genome Initiative of 2011 aimed to halve that, with no evidence it succeeded [secondhand]); lithium-ion batteries were proposed in the mid-1970s and became widespread in the late 1990s [secondhand: White House 2011]. AI compresses only the first stage: Berkeley's A-Lab synthesized 41 of 58 targets in 17 days, but only 37% of 355 recipes succeeded [secondhand: relayed from a 2023 paper].
    • Verification is fast, delivery slow: LK-99 went up on arXiv on 2023-07-22 and was refuted by several labs within about 3-4 weeks [secondhand].
    • Fusion: the US National Ignition Facility (NIF) produced 8.6 MJ with a target gain of 4.13 on 2025-04-07 [secondhand: LLNL annual report]; that is target gain, and the facility draws far more energy from the grid than it produces [not re-checked]; SPARC targets first plasma and Q>1 in 2027 [secondhand]; ITER deuterium-tritium operation in 2039 (2024 baseline) [secondhand].
    • The grid: large transformer lead times about 128 weeks (Wood Mackenzie 2025Q2), reported at 30-36 months in 2026 [secondhand]; interconnection median > 5 years [verified]; GE Vernova's 2028 turbine capacity of 24GW against a backlog plus slot reservations of 116GW [verified].
    • Drugs: a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine had its first positive Phase III on 2026-08-19 (recurrence-free survival, distant-metastasis-free survival); overall survival is not out and it is not approved [secondhand]; as of 2026-07 no AI-designed drug has FDA approval, and rentosertib registered a Phase III in 2026-07 [secondhand].
    • The bills: most of an energy bill is capital, transmission and capacity, not fuel; "marginal cost going to zero" does not mean "power prices going to zero."
  • Indicators and dates: SPARC Q>1 (target 2027, review 2027-12-31); count of room-temperature superconductivity replications (event); transformer lead times (around 2027-01); LBNL interconnection report (around 2027-05); full cancer-vaccine data and filing [not found: conference date].

15. Dario's zeroth world: the divergence is real, the order and the bill are not

Dario is right that AI's gains will pool first in a small group; we reject his assumptions that the growth arrives smoothly and the budget balances itself. On our main line, the zeroth world forms on paper first, is marked down by the credit cliff, and is bounded by shareholder and licensee lists, not by the Bay Area's map.

His words are concrete. His remedy: a government role in distribution, since "the budget may balance without us doing anything because there's so much growth" [verified: WSJ video 2026-01-20, auto-captions].

The statement speaks for an IPO and for a policy seat. Asked about listing this year, he cited "very high capital demands" beyond what private markets can supply; the nightmare presupposes an enormous pie, so the warning also prices the growth. His first step is measuring the transition with his own company's index; whoever measures feeds policy its data.

We take three pieces of logic. Gaps in diffusion speed split one country, startups from incumbents and state from state: the distribution side of our point that growth-rate gaps trigger the deep fall. High growth and high unemployment can coexist, so growth is no all-clear for jobs. His forecast that redistribution turns bipartisan within a year or two matches our call that "AI is taking our jobs" tops the 2028 election.

We discard two narratives, for one mechanism. Run his numbers backward: if the rest grows its usual 2%, sixty to eighty percent of national growth comes from about 3% of the population [estimate]. That cannot be mostly wages; it is mostly corporate profit, reaching people as unrealized equity gains. Expensing of capital spending shields profit (corporate income tax -15.6% in FY2026 [verified: MTS]), equity gains go untaxed until sold, and 10% unemployment shrinks the wage base. So the budget does not balance itself; distribution runs on deficits and printed money, our Act Two. His own essay that month names "progressive taxation" as the natural response [verified: darioamodei.com 2026-01]. Hence the border is not geographic: the Bay Area is merely where the people on the shareholder and licensee lists live.

Back on our chain, it is not a new road. The full version sits inside intelligence first (35%); the mild version on the third road (10 to 15%): credit clears only the heaviest borrowers, no recession, and any rise in unemployment follows the technology. On the main line (a credit event first, about 65%), the labs' listings put the zeroth world's paper on public markets; the AI-stock fall from autumn 2027 into 2028 marks it down, postponing the decoupling, not cancelling it; after AGI (median fourth quarter 2028) it regrows around the bottlenecks and the licensed tier. Both sides share the political clock; they differ on why jobs are lost.

Two dates tell the worlds apart. The monthly jobs report (next on 2026-11-06): unemployment above 5% (4.2% today) in a growing economy is his world, and the distribution fight comes 2-3 years early; unemployment rising with a recession is ours. The FY2027 federal statement (mid-October 2027): a deficit under 1.7 trillion dollars with corporate tax recovering is his self-balancing budget; interest above 21% of revenue is ours.
