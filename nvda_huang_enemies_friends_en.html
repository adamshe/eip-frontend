1. Conclusion: Three Days

On October 29, 2026, according to reports, the New York Times first disclosed that he had become a target of a foreign intelligence operation. On November 8, Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation, overseen by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel; the same statement also said the board has confidence in him and his management team. On November 11, Jensen Huang resigned from every post. On November 12, the company filed. The company's own statements and filings confirm all three dates. One of the most valuable companies in the world lost its founder in three days, and this report infers that those three days left him no room to fight back.

The public reason was the trigger; the powder keg was the rent, which is to say Nvidia's pricing power. A distributed copy of the company's results release reported a second-quarter gross margin of 75.0%; on that figure, Nvidia keeps USD 75 of gross profit from every USD 100 a customer pays (calculated). This report infers that the trigger decided only the day the departure came, not who stood on which side. By the frame this report sets, that was decided by the rent: who collects it and who pays it.

The record says one thing; the public accepted another. The stated reason in the board's November 8 statement is reporting that he had become a target of a foreign intelligence operation; the November 12 departure announcement gives no reason at all. Both originals are confirmed. Above the record sits the interpretation of editorials and columns, and above that the version the public finally accepted, which several media reports render as a matter of personal conduct. This report goes into none of its details and looks only beneath the iceberg.

Beneath the iceberg is force, not morality. Reasons and morals belong to the layer of interpretation; the outcome is the resultant of each camp's real strength, and the size of that resultant is written in the speed of the outcome. Three days, this report infers, means the force behind the outcome was far larger than the public reason: it had been building and needed only an opening, and the public reason supplied it. This report covers only camps and structure; it does not judge whether any party waited for or chose the timing.

This report sets the list as follows, sorted by economic position. The enemies come in four columns: the cloud giants who pay the rent, the US hardware rivals who compete for it, the national-interest side, and a media camp of capital made up of large outlets. The friends are of two kinds: the Taiwanese foundry and Korean memory makers that collect rent alongside Nvidia, and a weak chorus of voices (Elon Musk, Lex Fridman and the employees who back him). The balance is one-sided (inferred). Friends have a voice; enemies have the votes. The enemies also have the money, the orders, the procedure and the media.

By this report's reckoning (inferred), the two camps were lined up before the trigger came, and the readings were in plain sight. Take the payers' concentration, a first-quarter reading: the company's filing confirmed three direct customers at 21%, 17% and 16% of revenue, a combined 54% (calculated). Their substitution was already under way: their own chips were reportedly in production or in progress on TSMC 3-nanometer in 2026. The rent-sharing tie shows in two January 2026 reports: Nvidia is about 22% of TSMC's 2026 revenue, an analyst estimate, and about two thirds of its next-generation HBM (high-bandwidth memory) demand is expected to go to SK hynix. The national-interest side moved early too: according to reports, the three House committees' joint letter is dated November 4, and other reports say it was made public on the 5th; its subject is foreign infiltration of US technology and the company's counter-espionage measures, ahead of the board's November 8 announcement. The large outlets are the exception. Every stance piece this report could date was reportedly published on November 9 or later; their position before the event lay in their ownership, not in their articles (inferred).

As for a comeback, the road back to Nvidia is harder. The other report published on 2026-11-14 estimates his chance of returning to Nvidia at about 4% by the end of 2027 and about 7% by the end of 2029, and the rent-chain view points the same way (inferred). For founding or leading a company valued at USD 10 billion or more, the rent-sharing suppliers and capital providers are ready-made friends; even so, this report judges that path very unlikely, below that report's estimate, and the most likely outcome is retirement or remaining a shareholder only (both inferred).

2. Method: Two Tools and One Lens

Mao Zedong opened his essay Analysis of the Classes in Chinese Society by asking who the enemies are and who the friends are, and called it "a question of the first importance for the revolution"; the essay then places each class by its economic position. This report borrows that ruler and swaps in a single test, Nvidia's pricing power: those who collect rent on it stand on one side, those who pay it on the other. The test is set by this report.

The second tool comes from the 1937 essay On Contradiction: a situation holds several contradictions; one decides the whole and the others follow it. Here, this report infers, the principal contradiction is the rent, and the public reason is the trigger, the secondary side.

Gross margin measures the rent. A distributed copy of the company's release reported that for the second quarter of fiscal 2027, ended 2026-07-26, gross margin was 75.0% on both the US generally accepted accounting principles basis and the non-GAAP basis; the company guided the third quarter to 74.0%, plus or minus 0.5 percentage points.

This report sets out four layers to what "happened", each open to manipulation. At the bottom is reality, the event itself, visible only from a 360-degree full view, where every participant is subjective. Above it is the record (filings, statements and timelines), whose content is chosen by whoever holds the right to write it; here the record is the board statement and the departure filing. Above that is interpretation, what media, analysts and the parties say about the record, supplied by whoever owns a mouthpiece; the large outlets' editorials and columns live here. At the top is acceptance, the version the public ends up believing, here a matter of personal conduct, so he had to go; this report repeats none of its details. This report works at the layer of reality, on the balance of forces beneath the iceberg.

Sorting follows position, not personal ties. Parties on the rent chain (payers, competitors for the rent and rent-sharing suppliers) are sorted by economic position; individuals off the chain, meaning public supporters and employees, by their public stance; the national-interest side by how its interest relates to the pricing power; and the large outlets by their published stance. For the outlets, the stance is reported, the link from owner to stance is inferred, and The Economist's stance is set by this report. Each party goes to one of three sides, enemy, friend or middle, and any party whose stance was not found is left unsorted; that rule, too, is set by this report.

What separates this report from the other report published the same day is the test. The other report, published on 2026-11-14, sorted parties by their interest in his return or in a new venture; it put the cloud firms on both sides, because they depend on Nvidia's products while building substitutes, and put the supply chain in the middle. This report sorts by who collects the rent and who pays it, and on that test, set by this report, the cloud firms are payers and enemies, and the foundry and memory makers are rent-sharing suppliers and friends.

3. The Rent Chain: Where the Money Comes From and Where It Goes

The rent, this report infers, flows from the payers to Nvidia and from there holds up TSMC and the HBM makers through orders and prices; the three share one interest in the pricing power.

The paying end is highly concentrated. The company's first-quarter filing confirmed three direct customers at 21%, 17% and 16% of total revenue, a combined 54% (calculated); the second-quarter filing confirmed five direct customers at 22%, 14%, 13%, 11% and 10% of accounts receivable on 2026-07-26, a combined 70% (calculated). The company does not name these customers, and a direct customer is not always the end buyer; the buyers behind them are inferred to be mostly cloud giants. The collecting end is Nvidia, whose reading is the gross margin in the previous section.

TSMC also makes the payers' own chips. A single aggregator source reported that Google TPU Ironwood, Amazon Trainium 3 and Microsoft Maia 200 all use TSMC's 3-nanometer process, and that several generations of Meta MTIA are in progress.

The view that TSMC's pricing power is built around Nvidia is likewise inferred. The relationship is not black and white: the suppliers collect from every customer, the cloud giants with their own chips included, but Nvidia comes first.

5. Balance of Forces: The Friends Have a Voice, the Enemies Have the Votes

The friends are very weak and the enemies very strong (inferred). The balance comes apart cleanly: the friends are weak in three places, the enemies strong in four.

The friends' first weakness is that a voice does not convert into a vote. Musk stands in the same position, running a giant listed company of his own: his 2018 Tesla pay package reportedly went through shareholder litigation, was rescinded by the Delaware Court of Chancery in 2024 and was reinstated by the Delaware Supreme Court on 2025-12-19. His support, this report infers, is the sympathy of a peer. Lex Fridman, the venture investor, the hedge fund manager and the employees who back him are small figures as far as the board is concerned, and theirs is moral sympathy. Their statements are posts and paraphrases, some confirmed in the original and some only reported; they carry no vote on the Nvidia board, no committed capital and no procedural power.

The second weakness is that the rent-sharing suppliers are silent. No public statement on his departure since 2026-11-08 by TSMC, SK hynix, Samsung or Foxconn was found. The reason, this report infers, is that they hold no seat on the Nvidia board and also supply the cloud giants who pay the rent, so taking a public side costs them alone; their strongest support is capacity priority, and today that priority goes to Nvidia the company.

The third weakness is that employees have no collective vote. Their equity value sits inside the company, and unvested equity keeps them in place (inferred); no named or collective public statement was found.

The enemies' first strength is money and orders. Calculated from the filings, three direct customers made up 54% of first-quarter revenue and five made up 70% of accounts receivable on 2026-07-26; that the buyers behind them are mostly cloud giants is inferred. The payers reportedly keep paying while pushing their own chips on TSMC 3-nanometer.

The second strength is procedure. Three House committees have reportedly sent two rounds of letters and asked for a closed-door briefing on 2026-11-18; the committees are inferred to hold subpoena power. A US senator was reported on 2026-11-10 to have called for his resignation.

The third strength is votes. The company's filing confirms that the board accepted his resignation as president, chief executive and director and appointed a board chair and an interim chief executive. Reportedly, he held about 3.58% of the shares as of 2026-03-23, and the company has a single class of common stock.

The fourth strength is the media. The Economist ran several pieces arguing that he should go; this report did not retrieve the originals, so the item is set by this report. Reportedly, the Wall Street Journal editorial "Of Course Jensen Huang Had to Go" endorsed the outcome after he left, and several Bloomberg Opinion columns criticized him and the company's governance, though this report saw only their headlines.

The media camp, this report infers, belongs to capital. The three outlets' owners are large financial or media capital: reportedly, The Economist's owners are Exor at about 43.4% and Smith Financial at about 26.7%, the Wall Street Journal sits under News Corp, and Bloomberg sits under Bloomberg L.P., in which Michael Bloomberg holds about 88%. These owners sit with the institutional shareholders and the board, not with the founder; the step from owner position to editorial stance is inferred by this report, which does not judge whether the outlets coordinated in any way. Among the other outlets, Fortune leaned to the middle, Reuters was neutral and CNBC carried news only, each confirmed in the original or reported; the stances of the Financial Times, New York Times, Washington Post, Barron's and Forbes were not found, which does not mean they published nothing.

Even the voices are unequal. The friends speak through individual posts; the enemies speak through editorial pages and columns, backed by owners and distribution. Beyond voice, the enemies also hold the votes, the orders and the procedure, and the friends do not (inferred).

6. United Front: Two Paths

This report infers that no front can be formed on the path back to Nvidia, and that on the path to a new company the friends are ready-made but the odds of success are very small.

To return to Nvidia, he could unite the employees who back him and would need to win over the institutional holders, whose votes decide the board; the other report published on 2026-11-14 calculated that the ten largest institutions hold 33.45% together. The difficulty is that no party can be isolated. The payers hold more than half of revenue (calculated and inferred); Congress holds procedure, as the originals confirm; and the media camp stands with the board and the institutional shareholders (inferred). The rent-sharing suppliers have no votes to cast on this path. That is why this report's conclusion backs the other report's direction.

Founding or leading a company valued at USD 10 billion or more would mean an entirely different front; everything in this paragraph is inferred. He could unite the rent-sharing suppliers (the makers of leading-edge wafers, advanced packaging and HBM) with capital providers such as sovereign funds and venture capital. He could win over the cloud giants as buyers of a second source, and engineers too, though non-solicitation terms bind them; those terms were not found. He would need to isolate the congressional line by choosing a direction that does not overlap with controlled chips. The media camp is not on his side, and this path is argued with orders and capacity, not editorials.

This path is very unlikely, and the reason lies in his own balance sheet. The other report published on 2026-11-14 calculated that he holds about 3.58% of the shares, worth about USD 144.7 billion at the 2026-11-12 close. A USD 10 billion company, even if he owned all of it, would be less than one tenth of that stake (also calculated) and would add little. The most likely outcome, this report infers, is retirement or remaining a shareholder only. That report estimates this path at about 10% by 2027-12-31 and 25% by 2029-12-31; this report judges that too high and gives no number of its own.

8. What This Means for Readers Following the Company: The Man Is Gone, the Rent Remains

The man is gone; the rent remains. The rent chain keeps running, and what matters is the size of the rent, not who holds the seat. This report infers that the trigger decided the day of the departure and that the media bear on whether the man can come back, but that neither changes the size of the rent; the direction from here follows the resultant force, not the public reason.

The size of the rent shows in gross margin. The company reportedly guided third-quarter gross margin to 74.0%; which side of that line the actual figure lands on is the most direct reading of the pricing power.

The ceiling on the rent shows in the payers' substitution. The cloud giants' own chips are reportedly in production or in progress on TSMC 3-nanometer, and the pace of that production decides how much the payers are still willing to pay.

The other side of the rent shows in the suppliers' priority. Who gets advanced packaging and HBM capacity first is the supply-chain reading of the pricing power, and the current readings come from reports and estimates. If that priority loosens, the change in pricing power shows up in the supply chain first (inferred).

What the congressional line means for the company depends, this report infers, on whether it moves from the man to the company. This report gives no probability of its own.
