Where Jensen Huang Lands

1. The First Question: The Supply Side and the Demand Side

Three days. Three days after the board announced an investigation, Jensen Huang resigned every post at Nvidia. One of the world's most valuable companies lost its founder, just like that. The board accepted his resignation, the major shareholders said nothing, and the Taiwanese and Korean suppliers who got rich off him, TSMC and SK hynix among them, kept silent.

He overestimated his allies and underestimated his opponents.

In 1925 Mao Zedong opened Analysis of the Classes in Chinese Society by asking who the enemies are and who the friends are. He called it "a question of the first importance for the revolution". Applied to Nvidia, sorting enemies from friends takes one test: does a party stand on the supply side or the demand side? The supply side is Nvidia and its Taiwanese and Korean suppliers. The demand side is the buyers of chips: cloud giants, model companies and nations. This report jokingly calls the extra money Nvidia makes from its pricing power a "tax", because those who pay it have no choice.

The answer first. Reportedly he was the target of a foreign intelligence operation; that was the cause above the iceberg. This report infers that the cause beneath the iceberg is the demand side hitting back at the supply side. The demand was his own creation. He turned a buyer's market into a seller's market and collected an excess tax. When the buyers could no longer carry it, they wanted the pricing power back, and he was their first target. The game is zero-sum; in the language of Mao's On Contradiction, the tax is the principal contradiction. The man is gone, and the excess tax can no longer be collected.

What happened in those three days?

2. Three Days: The Cause and the Iceberg

First, the timeline. According to reports, the New York Times disclosed on October 29 that he had become a target of a foreign intelligence operation. On November 8 the board announced an independent investigation, and the same statement expressed confidence in him. On November 11 he resigned every post. The next day the company filed, and the announcement gave no reason.

That is the record. Truth has four layers, reality, record, interpretation and acceptance, and each can drift. The record speaks of foreign intelligence. Several media reports say the public accepted a matter of personal conduct, whose details this report does not repeat. Beneath the iceberg lies force, not morality.

This report judges that a three-day exit says the force far exceeded the public reason. That force had been building, and once the gap opened it rushed in. Section 6 weighs another explanation. This report covers only camps and structure, and does not judge who raised the allegations. How was that force built up?

3. Five Moves: How Five Trillion Was Played

It was built up in five moves he played himself. In this report's view, the first four were fine moves and the fifth was a risky one.

One, borrowing the east wind, as the Chinese saying goes. This wave was lit by ChatGPT, which was reportedly released on November 30, 2022. Model companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic are indirect demand, and he rode their momentum.

Two, placing stones. Direct demand is the four big cloud firms ordering hardware, and at that layer he raised up two stones of his own. Nvidia reportedly bought into CoreWeave in 2023 and put in another USD 2 billion in January 2026; per Nebius's announcement, Nvidia took part in its December 2024 financing. The two stones stirred up the big firms' order of battle and forced an arms race.

Three, locking up supply. Making things is usually a buyer's market: buyers squeeze prices and makers fight for orders. TSMC expanded cautiously, and he booked its advanced capacity solid. In this report's view, he set up a game that turned the Taiwanese and Korean makers into sellers in a seller's market. By reported estimates, Nvidia booked more than half of TSMC's 2026 advanced-packaging capacity, and about two-thirds of demand for HBM4, the new generation of high-bandwidth memory (HBM), goes to SK hynix. Their capacity became scarce, and the makers got to pick customers and set prices. Memory is a cyclical commodity, yet SK hynix's operating margin reached 80% (see the table in Section 5). The food chain was turned around. No one else could have done it.

Four, technology pushed to the limit.

For an ordinary company, pulling off any one of these is already the ceiling; he pushed all four to the limit. Only one of the four is technology. The other three are the running of demand, of the field and of the supply chain, and even the suppliers whose capacity he had booked solid stood on his side. Nvidia's market value reportedly topped USD 5 trillion for the first time on October 29, 2025.

Five, China: the endgame, and the fork in the road. America was the main board; China was only a corner to close out. Per the annual report, before exports were restricted, China (including Hong Kong) was about a quarter of revenue. Reportedly, he called it a market of about USD 50 billion within two to three years, and said being shut out would be a tremendous loss. He wanted that corner too: reportedly, the US government allowed H200 sales to China with a 25% cut, and Nvidia applauded. In this report's view, the move set his own house on fire: to close out a corner, he overturned the main board. That fork is open to question.

The game was won, and the tax came in. Could the buyers carry it?

4. From Alliance to Zero-Sum

At first they could. In the first stage, buyers and sellers stood on one front, and both wanted to make the industry bigger. In the second stage, the bottleneck appeared: the tax in the middle had grown too heavy, and the buyers on the front line could no longer keep going. Here the alliance turned zero-sum.

A word in fairness first: making money for shareholders is a chief executive's job. From a god's-eye view, though, Nvidia is a pump on the whole compute chain. Per its results release, the fiscal 2027 second-quarter gross margin was 75.0%. Put simply: for goods that cost a dollar to make, TSMC charges three dollars and Nvidia charges four. Calculated from the releases, gross profit over the last four quarters was about USD 226.2 billion: that is a year's tax.

It draws twice. First comes cash flow, from the buyers in an arms race: nations, companies, even individuals. Then comes valuation: a market-cap aggregator reported Nvidia's market value at about USD 3.93 trillion on November 13, calculated at about 20 times four quarters of net income. Shareholders, index funds and pensions buying by weight among them, have prepaid future tax. Every layer of the chain is paying.

The buyers never say it aloud, but they have a charge in mind: "price gouging". The charge comes from no legal code, only from the procurement table.

The tax ultimately has to be paid out of downstream revenue. The two leading downstream model companies reportedly earn about $115 billion a year combined; on that basis, that is about half of a year's tax. The bases differ, so the figures indicate scale only. Reportedly, Alphabet's free cash flow turned negative last quarter, and it has borrowed and sold shares. Users' money cannot pay this tax. When money tightens, the payers squeeze the largest, most compressible line first. This report infers that the man who set prices face to face takes the first hit.

Not all of the tax stays at Nvidia. Calculated from the filings, three direct customers made up 54% of first-quarter revenue, mostly cloud giants behind them. The tax flows from them to Nvidia, then props up TSMC and the HBM makers through orders. Others share in this tax too, so why was he the only one besieged?

5. The Original Sin: Why Nvidia

The answer is not how deep the monopoly runs. TSMC shares in this tax too, yet drew no siege. TrendForce reported that it held 72.5% of global foundry revenue in the second quarter of 2026, a more textbook monopoly. Calculated from its releases, its gross profit over the last four quarters was about USD 103 billion, under half of Nvidia's USD 226.2 billion.

TSMC's chairman and chief executive put it plainly on the 2026-07-16 earnings call. According to a reprinted transcript, he said: "I don't want to squeeze them out from the market." "Them" means customers: the line just before says the customer has to succeed.

Chapter 67 of Laozi's Tao Te Ching lists three treasures: compassion, frugality, and "not daring to be first under heaven". TSMC's restraint rests on its chief executive's own words; matching it to Laozi is this report's judgment. It is a philosophy of survival, set against the way of living that pushes appetite and ability to the limit, and no side is taken here.

SK hynix's last-quarter operating income was close to Nvidia's, but it rose within a year. At an estimated exchange rate, its four-quarter operating income totals about USD 151 billion, still below Nvidia's USD 197.6 billion. Its profits also sit inside Nvidia's costs.

Nvidia, by contrast, sets prices face to face, with its chief executive out front himself. TSMC leaves customers feeling they were not squeezed out. Both are pumps, and the "charge" landed only on Nvidia.

So the "original sin" has two parts: the total pumped, scale times margin, and standing at the very front. Holding both, he became a thorn in the side of all his opponents. The target was up; who were the opponents?

6. Enemies, Friends and the Counterattack

By position, both camps were set before anything happened. The buyers were already concentrated, and their own chips were already under way. Supply-chain reports in January 2026 had already set out the ranking of the Taiwanese and Korean suppliers. Reportedly, three House committees wrote on November 4, after the first report, which shows only that Congress moved before the board.

The enemy side has its reasons, so first, its case. The chief executive of the world's most critical chip company was reportedly a target of a foreign intelligence operation. That is a real national-security risk. The board has a fiduciary duty, and an investigation is its job. The congressional letters were about precisely foreign infiltration and the company's counter-espionage measures, not pricing, and that point must be granted. What the national-interest side cares about, this report infers, is key technology and pricing power concentrated in one person's hands.

That case explains an investigation, not the silence: why did none of the board, the major shareholders or the Taiwanese and Korean suppliers speak up for him? Look at the incentives. The demand side wants lower prices, and its first target is the man who set prices face to face and built the demand besides. Pressure from the state and the media then made him the lightning rod. The board and major shareholders collect the tax too, yet they did not stand in the way; perhaps they hoped to let him go and keep the tax. This report holds that it cannot be kept: the demand was his creation, and without him the excess tax will not hold.

Beyond the reasons, look at force. The votes sit with the board and shareholders. Reportedly he himself holds only about 3.58%, and the company has a single class of common stock. The three things that move those votes are all in his opponents' hands: orders, procedure and the media. Orders: the buyers hold more than half of revenue. Procedure: Congress has sent two rounds of letters and asked for a closed-door briefing on November 18. The media: the editorials and columns sided with his going; whether that links to their owners, this report does not judge.

He lost by underestimating his opponents. This report infers that the buyers could not do without his goods, so to his face they only flattered him and said everyone depended on him for a living; anger they dared not voice was still anger. The foreign-intelligence line means investigation and evidence, and could drag on for perhaps two years, like a lawsuit; the personal-conduct line was over in days. Such an opening was exactly what the side hitting back was waiting for. This report has no evidence that any party had a hand in raising the allegations, and implies nothing about whether they are true; this is an inference from incentives and speed.

His own political defenses were thin. Reportedly, Nvidia spent about USD 4.95 million on federal lobbying in 2025, less than a fifth of Meta's USD 26.29 million; per Federal Election Commission records, the company's political action committee first filed only in August 2026. He had staked his political capital on his relationship with one man, the president, and no one was watching his opponents for him.

Another explanation is that the investigation found undisclosed hard evidence within those days. No record of it was found, and three days earlier the board still expressed confidence in him. Both explanations fit the public record, and the evidence cannot tell them apart. This report infers from speed and camps and leans toward the counterattack explanation; once hard evidence is made public, this judgment is void. Congress and the press had moved; where were the friends?

7. Silence: Keep Quiet and Get Rich

The friends were off the chain, with only a voice. Musk's support is a peer's sympathy, and Lex Fridman speaks from moral sympathy. Fridman's pinned post reads: "NVIDIA letting Jensen go is a huge mistake, for America and for humanity."

The Taiwanese and Korean suppliers on the chain said nothing. No public statement from TSMC, SK hynix, Samsung or Foxconn can be found. An old Chinese saying fits: keep quiet and get rich. Their seller's market was his making; still they say nothing. Theirs is a cyclical business: they earn as much as they can in a boom, sit out a few lean quarters, and fill the hole with the boom's profit. This is a boom, and they hold no board seats; none of them speaks up for one man. This report infers that the silence is itself a statement.

Earning more is a company's job, and no one can fault it; in this report's view, though, their attitudes to expansion come in two kinds. Each memory maker, on its own reckoning, holds off as long as it can: per its release, SK hynix sets the timing of its investment flexibly, and long-term contracts lock in over half of its 2027 and 2028 capacity. Reportedly, those contracts carry prepayments of 10% to 30% and a price floor.

TSMC is cautious in what it says and cautious in what it does. Reportedly, although its capital budget was raised to USD 60 billion to 64 billion, spending still follows customer demand and does not run ahead of it. This report's view is that its caution serves the long term: it stretches out the cycle and holds a high but reasonable margin. Dropping it would serve short-term gain at its own expense. Nvidia's high margin, by contrast, is in this report's view not reasonable.

In this report's view, all of this is chokepoint business: done quietly, never said out loud. Friends silent, opponents holding leverage: can he go back?

8. Where He Lands: The Man Is Gone, and the Tax Can No Longer Be Collected

He cannot. No front can be formed, with the buyers, Congress and the media on the other side. This report infers that he will most likely retire for good. Was this ending inevitable? Not necessarily. Had Nvidia earned less, or not stood at the very front, the other side might not have gathered so completely. He might still have got into trouble, but one of the board, the major shareholders or the Taiwanese and Korean suppliers might have spoken up for him.

The pump stays with the company, but the man who built the demand is gone. What the demand side gained is the departure of the toughest price-setter. This report infers that the excess tax will fade gradually, while normal profit remains.

On Mao's first question, he got both halves wrong. He overestimated his allies and underestimated his opponents.
